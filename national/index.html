Hormuz — NEW

FOUR TANKERS IN THREE DAYS

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Maritime intel firm Marisks identified three Liberian-flagged tankers struck by unknown projectiles while transiting Hormuz on Tuesday — Al Ruwais, Mersin Prosperity (both ADNOC Logistics & Services-managed), and Sinbad — all running with AIS transponders off (Reuters). (HIGH)
  • Thursday, UKMTO reported a fourth tanker struck by an unknown projectile — fire aboard, crew safe, damage and environmental impact unknown. (HIGH)
  • The NYT reports Western security data showing Iran launching ~30 drone attacks and ~10 anti-ship missile attacks per week on commercial ships since early August, often missing; at least 4 ships hit in the past 10 days. The IRGC publicly confirmed near-daily strikes on state TV — "every 24 hours." (HIGH)
  • The desk's read: the AIS-off detail is the tell — operators now transit dark through the world's most important chokepoint. The question isn't whether strikes continue; it's which one produces the first insurance-market rupture.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Who fired on each tanker — no group claimed responsibility; whether any vessel will be total-loss or declared a hazard.

Energy · the diesel lever — MIDDAY

THE DIESEL ULTIMATUM BECOMES A G7 DEAL

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Reuters exclusive: the Trump administration told Germany and France to draw down emergency diesel inventories — the ask is 120 million barrels of diesel over six months — or face a potential US diesel export ban (three sources). Motive: lower US fuel prices ahead of the Nov 3 midterms; diesel hit a record $6.23/gal in mid-September, up 55%+ since the Iran war began. (HIGH)
  • Energy Secretary Wright said on Fox News Friday he was "highly confident" of European cooperation and expected "announcements soon." (HIGH)
  • The EU "fully rejects" the threat — Commission spox Anna-Kaisa Itkonen: "A ban would not be beneficial to anyone." An EU energy taskforce met Friday morning; the French proposal on the table: 50M barrels of diesel plus 50M of crude. (HIGH)
  • Midday: after a morning of rejection — Itkonen adding a ban would "undermine our trust in the United States as a reliable partner" — France chaired a G7 videoconference Friday and leaders agreed to a coordinated 100-million-barrel release over four months through the IEA, beginning immediately, including a "frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days" by G7 members and partners. (CNN, USA Today, The Times) (HIGH)
  • Trump posted on Truth Social that Europe agreed to release "a massive amount" of diesel and that "the process will begin immediately"; he spoke with Macron Thursday evening and with G7 leaders Friday morning (White House official via CNN). The G7 statement also commits members to refrain from export restrictions on energy between G7 countries — the US ban threat is off the table among G7. (HIGH on the statement and the post)
  • Macron: the G7 will coordinate to restore full freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz, where "more than three quarters" of pre-war volumes are now flowing. (The Times) (HIGH)
  • Caveats: Germany has been reluctant to release any barrels (POLITICO via France 24, MEDIUM); IEA chief Birol said about a third of the 400 million barrels members agreed to release in March has not yet reached the market (France 24, MEDIUM-HIGH).
  • The desk's midday read: the ultimatum became the deal — Europe traded barrels for the ban being taken off the table, and routed the release through the IEA so it reads as coordination rather than capitulation.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The final diesel-vs-crude breakdown of the 100M barrels; whether Germany participates; whether the frontloaded 20-day release actually lands; whether the ban threat stays shelved.
