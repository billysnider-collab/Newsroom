Pipeline attack · Trump's call

Trump: Iran "probably" behind the Saudi pipeline attack

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Trump, in Dublin on Saturday: "I think they are, probably they are," when asked whether Iran was behind Thursday's drone attack on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline (Reuters, CNN).
  • Saudi foreign ministry confirmed Friday: the East-West Pipeline in the Riyadh and Madinah regions was hit Thursday by several drones "coming from Iraq" — injuries and some damage; shut down as a precaution (Saudi Energy Ministry).
  • Satellite imagery taken Friday appears to show extensive fire damage at one pumping station (CNN).
  • The Islamic Resistance in Iraq — the umbrella group of Iran-backed militias — denied responsibility; Iraq launched an investigation and dismissed a commander in Maysan province (Times).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Attribution: Saudi reporting says the drones came from Iraq; Iran has not claimed the attack; an Iraqi militia umbrella denied it. Trump's "probably" is an assessment, not evidence.
  • Le Monde attributes the pipeline bombing to the Houthis — conflicts with the Saudi/Iraq framing; treat both as single-source until they converge.

Washington · Riyadh

The ask Trump said no to — twice

What we know CONFIRMED

  • MBS called Trump at least twice on Thursday urging U.S. strikes on the Houthis as they advanced down the Red Sea coast (Axios via Reuters; CNN source). Trump declined.
  • Washington told Riyadh it would not take direct military action "for now" but would help with intelligence sharing and targeting (Reuters). CENTCOM chief Adm. Brad Cooper flew to Saudi Arabia Thursday for coordination.
  • Trump said the Houthis phoned his administration indicating they do not want the U.S. directly involved: "They would much prefer not having us involved, and they're letting most ships go through. There's just one country that they're not too happy with" (Reuters).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • What "help with intelligence sharing and targeting" means in practice — the scope is undefined.
  • The Houthi phone claim is Trump's account alone; no independent confirmation.

Yemen · Red Sea

Mokha, Mayun, and the artillery-range threat

What we know CONFIRMED

  • After seizing Mokha's port Thursday, Houthi fighters took the island of Mayun (Perim) in the Bab el-Mandeb strait — from it, the African coast is ~20 km away (Le Monde).
  • A Houthi NCO on camera Friday: with artillery positions "here and there" along the coast, they can strike tankers from several directions — no missiles or drones needed. The strait carries ~12% of global trade.
  • Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said maritime navigation remains safe for all shipping except Saudi vessels under a previously announced ban.
  • Riyadh answered with airstrikes on the Mokha region; Yemeni government forces signaled they will try to retake the captured areas (Reuters).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether Saree's "safe except Saudi vessels" holds against actual transit counts next week.
  • A Yemeni counterpush — signaled, not yet launched.

Miami · NTSB

No brakes, no reversers — the 767 overrun's center of gravity

What we know CONFIRMED

  • FDR: no speed-brake or thrust-reverser deployment; "Too low, terrain" sounded repeatedly (investigator Chihoon Shin, Reuters).
  • One pilot flagged excessive speed through the last 1:42 of the CVR with no consistent response from the other pilot.
  • Aircraft: 32-year-old 767-300 (21 Air-operated Amazon Prime Air Flight 7598); overrun ~1,300 ft; struck a 7-person cleaning van.
  • 5 killed, 5 injured (NTSB Chair Homendy, USA Today).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Cause — NTSB explicitly refused to speculate.
  • Which pilot raised the speed concern — not disclosed.
  • The promised full CVR readout — still the next data point.
