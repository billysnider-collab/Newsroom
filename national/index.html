FOMC · Wednesday

Warsh hiked — unanimously. The dots say one more this year

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The FOMC voted 12-0 Wednesday for +25bp to 3.75–4.00% — the first hike since July 2023 and the first rate change since Dec 2025 (per WSJ, Reuters, CNN).
  • Warsh at the 2:30 presser: "Inflation is too high and has been for too long," "this committee will deliver price stability," and "I'm not in the forward guidance business."
  • The SEP pencils in one more hike this year: median end-2026 at 4.1% (from 3.8%); 12 see +25bp more, 4 see +50bp by year-end; no further hikes in 2027. Warsh submitted no dots.
  • Futures now ~50% odds of an October 28 hike — five days before the midterms. Reuters: the market prices three total hikes.
  • Retail sales ran hot Wednesday morning: +1.2% vs +0.8% expected, control group +1.4% — the largest in ~2 years — giving Warsh cover (per Reuters, WSJ). Capital Economics: the economy is "more than capable of handling higher rates."
  • The 10-year closed at 5.003% — the first close above 5% in 19 years; the 30-year at 5.3328% (off the 5.401% 19-year high). Dow -631 (-1.2%) to a 3-month low; S&P -0.4%; Nasdaq ~flat.
  • Trump on Truth Social: rates should be "1%, or less… LOWER THE INTEREST RATES… AND FAST!" To reporters: Warsh is "a good man" but he's got a "hostile board" — the FOMC raises "only for political reasons, and that's a raise against Trump" (per Barron's, Reuters).
  • Thursday's tape reversed Wednesday's selloff: Nasdaq +1.7%, S&P +1.1%, Dow +0.6% (+323) — tech led as the 10-year sank back BELOW 5% (~4.94%, down 8+bps) on the oil pullback and the BoE's gilt-sale pause (per Investopedia, Barron's). Boockvar: lower inflation expectations from the hike + the BoE QT pause cooled the global bond market.
  • FedWatch into Thursday's close: 53% odds of an October 28 hike, 87% of at least one hike by the December meeting — up from the morning's ~50% coin-flip.
  • Trump on Wednesday evening: "hopefully we are towards end" of the Iran war — even as the Saudi–Houthi front escalated (per Reuters).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether October delivers a hike; how Trump escalates — he "may not see another rate cut as president" (per Reuters' McGeever).
  • Whether Thursday's sub-5% move holds into Friday's BoJ decision, or the 10Y retests 5% (ING: 5.25% is the next target).

Russia · Ukraine · overnight

Russia hit Kyiv and Odesa — hours after the House passed the sanctions bill

What we know CONFIRMED

  • In the early hours Thursday, Russian strikes hit Kyiv and Odesa — Reuters: 18 injured and buildings damaged; Klitschko's tally updated to 18 injured including two children. The Kyiv Independent: 10 killed, 71 injured nationwide over the past day.
  • Hours earlier, the House passed the Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act 262-159 — authorizes tariffs up to 100% on China/India/buyers of Russian energy and sanctions on Russia's energy/defense/shadow fleet; now to Trump's desk (signature expected). Zelenskyy: it is "symbolic" it passed on a night of another Russian attack (per Reuters, WSJ).
  • Yaroslavl is now confirmed by Russia itself: the YANOS refinery (300 kb/d, co-owned by Rosneft and Gazprom Neft) halted crude processing Thursday after the overnight drone attack (per Reuters, four industry sources); governor Yevrayev on Telegram: 65 drones downed, no casualties, the refinery sustained damage and fires are being extinguished.
  • The damage: the AVT-3 primary crude distillation unit — roughly 40% of the plant's capacity — was damaged; AVT-4 was already under repair after the Aug 28 strike. Yaroslavl fuel was not offered on the domestic exchange Thursday. The diesel mechanics keep tightening: Russia is preparing to extend its diesel export ban through the end of October (per Vedomosti, via OilPrice).
  • Russia answered on the grid: its Defense Ministry says it hit a battery-storage park at Brovary near Kyiv and a power station feeding the Izmail port. Ukraine's Air Force: Russia fired 157 drones/missiles overnight; defenses downed 131.
  • Trump has not announced a signing date for the Graham bill (per rwtnews). The ceasefire-enforcement frame keeps proving itself: a bill that authorizes tariffs isn't a mechanism either — it's a threat with a pen. Until breaking a halt costs more than keeping it, both sides keep bombing.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The exact repair timeline at Yaroslavl — the plant has been a repeated drone target this year.
  • Whether Trump signs the Graham bill and whether he actually uses the secondary-tariff authority (Meeks: no guarantee).

UN · the findings

"Reasonable grounds" the US committed war crimes in Iran strikes

What we know CONFIRMED

  • A UN-backed mission of experts found "reasonable grounds" to believe the United States committed war crimes in two deadly Iran strikes — including one on a school in the southern city of Minab — and found grounds to believe Tehran committed crimes against humanity (per AP, Reuters).
  • The findings go to the UN Human Rights Council in Geneva. Caption it as "reasonable grounds / possible" — not a verdict.
  • The report names the Shajareh Tayyebeh Primary School in Minab — more than 150 people killed, including around 120 schoolchildren, according to Iranian officials — and a second strike on a sports centre in Lamerd that killed and injured 22 civilians (per Reuters). Casualty counts are attributed, not verified by the desk.
  • Neither the US nor Iranian permanent missions in Geneva responded to Reuters. The findings go before the UN Human Rights Council on Monday.
  • Day 201 of a war Congress voted three times to constrain, and now a UN fact-finding mission is putting the US and Iran in the same paragraph — "reasonable grounds" for war crimes on the US side, crimes against humanity on Tehran's.
  • The story isn't a verdict — it's that the institutional record is being written while the war is still going, which raises the cost of the next strike for everyone.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The US response (no official reaction in the pull); the mission's full report text; whether the HRC acts.

US–Iran war · Day 201

Trump: "hopefully we are towards end" — as the war escalates

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Trump said he hoped an end to the Iran war was near — on the same day Saudi aircraft pounded Yemen and Houthi drones/missiles hit Saudi cities (per Reuters), and two days after the Mecca drone attempt.
  • AP reports Riyadh asked France, Britain, Pakistan, and Egypt for air-defense support; US stocks are also dwindling after six months of the Iran war. Al-Maliki: Mecca is a "red line"; he promised "necessary deterrent measures."
  • Salalah talks are still postponed with no new date. Hope is not a mechanism.
  • Thursday's escalation: the first announced Saudi death — a Yemeni resident was killed in Taif by debris from an intercepted Houthi drone; a Saudi woman and a Pakistani resident were also injured. Riyadh says 86+ people were injured in Houthi attacks this month (per Reuters).
  • The Houthis released video they say shows a downed Saudi F-15 over Marib, claiming a "locally manufactured" surface-to-air missile. CNN geolocated the wreckage to Marib; the WSJ reports two people familiar with the incident say a Saudi plane was downed; Saudi Arabia and the Arab Coalition have not confirmed or refuted it — published as a claim, not a fact.
  • China privately pressed Tehran to rein in the Houthis after a Saudi appeal, per three Iranian sources (Reuters); Tehran's response: stability depends on ending the US-Israeli war on Iran.
  • Trump rebuffed Saudi pleas for military support against the Houthis (per Reuters) — and said Thursday he is approaching a "critical juncture" on whether to restart massive attacks on Iran. The US tightened its travel warning for Saudi Arabia: no government-employee travel within 20 miles of the Yemen border.
  • End-of-war talk during escalation is a tell — either Trump knows something coming or he's reading the election calendar.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • What "towards end" is based on — no announced talks or framework.
  • The IRGC Navy's claim that a Togo-flagged tanker caught fire in Hormuz after being hit for "illegal passage" — Iranian state media only, no confirmation; held out entirely.
  • The US answer to Riyadh's air-defense ask. All Iranian MQ-9 claims (no Pentagon confirmation) — held out entirely.

Energy · Day 7

Three pumping stations hit — the repair timeline just got murkier

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Reuters (Thursday): THREE pumping stations serving the 1,200 km East-West Pipeline were damaged in the Sept 11 attack — one more than the two previously assessed — per satellite-imagery analysis and three industry sources. Aramco and the Saudi government media office did not respond to requests for comment.
  • The repair timeline is now unclear: three sources say five to six weeks; another says partial pumping could resume sooner while repairs continue. The "half capacity within days" plan (MUFG citing media — secondhand) is a plan, not a fact.
  • The relief trade keeps working into the close: Brent ~$104 Thursday (-1.7%), WTI $101.25 (-1.2%) — the war premium keeps leaking out (per Investopedia ~4pm ET). Saudi is increasing shipments via Oman, with expanded crude offers through ship-to-ship transfers off Sohar — Reuters: the kingdom is offering MORE loadings to Asian refiners via Sohar STS (per Reuters, WSJ).
  • The physical bid underneath: no Saudi crude has left Red Sea ports since Saturday (satellite data); Yanbu ~15M bbl (Kpler); Aramco told some European customers no cargoes through mid-November (per CNN). API's +7.1M-bbl build; EIA forecasts ~$90/bbl average in 2H26, falling to $74 in 2027.
  • Saudi says the attack drones came from Iraq; Baghdad opened an investigation.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Which repair timeline is right — five to six weeks, or a partial resume sooner.
  • The first confirmed Sohar ship-to-ship loading — the physical proof of concept.

Trade · Canada–EU

Von der Leyen offers Canada a seat; Trump threatens Europe

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Von der Leyen proposed Canada as the EU's first-ever "associate member" in her State of the Union — Carney attended (per CNN, Reuters, WSJ).
  • Trump called it "laughable" and threatened "very serious tariffs" or to stop trading with Europe if he deems it a "hostile act." France's Haddad: the US has no veto over EU choices.
  • Separately, Trump moved to restrict Canadian goods from federal procurement. Context: 50% on ~$20B of Canadian goods last month; Canada's 15–50% retaliation on $20B of US goods Sept 8; Sept 29 import bans pending.
  • Only 20% of Americans support higher Canada tariffs (per Reuters/Ipsos), and Susan Collins calls the policy a "mistake" — the domestic politics are weakening while the trade war spreads to a second front.
  • This is the tariff war's expansion phase: the target pivots to a new partner, and the threat turns on the partner's partner.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • What "associate member" would legally mean; whether the Sept 29 bans get carve-outs.

Washington · Beijing

The GAIN AI Act — chips, China, and a canal

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The GAIN AI Act was introduced in the House hours after Trump said he discussed Nvidia processor access with Xi — it would limit US AI chip exports to China (Rep. Moolenaar, per Bloomberg Law).
  • China opened the Pinglu Canal Wednesday: 134.2 km, ¥72.7B, 5,000-ton vessels — the first national-level river-to-sea canal since 1949 (per TBS).
  • The AI-safety debate is still live in Washington: Altman posted that rapid AI progress could go "very badly" and humans could lose "control of the future," welcomed a federal frontier-AI framework — and said an OpenAI IPO "will not happen in 2026" (per Reuters/Fortune).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • GAIN's committee path and whether it survives contact with the Sept 24 Trump–Xi meeting (still on track per reporting).
