Black Sea — EVENING

A DRONE SINKS A SHIP IN BULGARIA'S WATERS — CREW MISSING

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Two ships were hit Tuesday in a combined air-and-sea drone attack about 70 nautical miles (112 km) off Byala, in Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone — the first such attack in Bulgarian waters, per shipping sources (Reuters). A NATO member's waters are now in the blast radius. (HIGH)
  • The sunk ship: a Togo-flagged merchant vessel, cargo undisclosed — its crew is missing, the count undisclosed, and a search is underway for the crew and any environmental threat. The second ship was carrying grain — its crew was rescued, two injured. (Reuters, HIGH)
  • Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev called the strike 'a gross violation of international law' — and said it was unclear whether the drones came from Russia or Ukraine. (HIGH)
  • Zelenskyy, in his nightly address: a joint Ukrainian-Bulgarian navy investigation showed this was a Russian combined attack using sea and aerial drones — 'The Ukrainian Defence Forces were conducting no operations in this part of the sea.' A maritime-security source's early assessment also pointed to Russia. Moscow has made no comment. Von der Leyen: 'Attacks on civilian shipping are unacceptable ... with lives already lost at sea.' (HIGH as stated positions)
  • The desk's read: the war's geography just expanded on camera — Radev said it himself: 'If this war is not brought to an end in time, it will continue to expand its geographical scope.' The attribution question is the HOLD that decides whether this is an incident or an escalation.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Who launched the drones — Russia or Ukraine (Radev: unclear; Zelenskyy: Russia; maritime-security source: probably Russia; Moscow: silent); the number of missing crew; the sunk ship's cargo; Zelenskyy's warning of a looming Russian 'massive strike' (single line in the reporting).

FZ1073 — EVENING

THE WHITE HOUSE SPLITS WITH ITSELF

What we know CONFIRMED

  • President Trump told reporters he “personally” believes Iran was behind last week's flydubai FZ1073 hijack attempt — “I do, I personally do” — giving no explanation. Hours earlier, Vice President JD Vance said he had seen no conclusive evidence of an Iranian role (per The Times). The White House is now on both sides of its own accusation. (HIGH)
  • The Australia thread thickened but stayed unverified: the FT reports the co-pilot, 29-year-old Omani Hamam al-Hammami, visited radical mosques and accessed extremist websites while in Australia, and that Australian intelligence services held a file on him — citing two people familiar with the investigation. Reuters could not immediately verify the FT report, and Australia's government did not respond outside business hours. No formal charges announced. (HOLD)
  • Netanyahu has said the co-pilot underwent radical Islamist indoctrination and was clearly suicidal, without saying when or where; he ordered the National Security Council to review all security aspects of foreign civil aviation into Israel, and questioned how a pilot from a country with no diplomatic relations with Israel was assigned to the Tel Aviv route (per Reuters). (MEDIUM-HIGH)
  • Carried from Monday: Reuters reports a source briefed on the UAE investigation said al-Hammami planned to crash the flight into Ben Gurion Airport, and two Israeli officials said he told questioning he planned it BEFORE flydubai hired him; the UAE Attorney-General stated Saturday he used an emergency crash axe in an attempted “terrorist act”. (HIGH as reported claims)
  • The record: enrolled at RMIT 2015–2017 with no completed engineering degree (per Reuters, HIGH); the Victorian JCTT probe (AFP spokesperson, HIGH). Most passengers were Israeli nationals; a reserve flydubai pilot landed the aircraft at Tabuk. UAE authorities and flydubai have not publicly disclosed the suspect's identity.
  • EVENING — a third investigative front: New Zealand police confirmed Tuesday the co-pilot spent 175 days in New Zealand — Nov 30, 2018 to May 23, 2019 — on a student visa, completing part of his flight training for a UK-based company that no longer operates there; police are reviewing records of the stay and say the review does not mean wrongdoing was found there. (Reuters, HIGH)
  • The desk's read: the Iran column is now a split inside the White House itself — the president asserts what the VP says doesn't exist. Until someone shows evidence, the accusation column stays empty and the president just walked into it.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether Iran was involved — the president believes, the VP has seen no evidence; whether the FT's mosques/websites/file claims are real; motive; formal charges; the UAE/Saudi confirmation of the Ben Gurion plan detail.

Hormuz — EVENING

THE TANKER WAR STOPS BEING BLOODLESS

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Monday was not quiet: the UKMTO logged five incidents Monday alone. The wave's first confirmed injuries landed Tuesday morning — twelve crew hurt on the Panama-flagged Aframax tanker On Peace, struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz Monday, fire in the engine room; 11 of the 12 injured are Indian nationals of a 19-person crew, evacuated with Omani assistance to Khasab, Oman, where they are receiving medical treatment (Reuters, AFP, India's foreign ministry statement Tuesday). (HIGH)
  • India condemned the attack: “We condemn the attack earlier today on a Panama-flagged commercial vessel MT On Peace... it was struck by a projectile while transiting the Strait of Hormuz,” the MEA said, calling the continuing incidents a “cause of deep concern.” The manager (Anglo-Eastern Tanker Management Hong Kong) and operator (ONEX DMCC) did not respond to Reuters. The vessel was last seen discharging fuel oil at Fujairah on August 26 with its transponder off (Kpler via Reuters).
  • The other Monday hit: Greek LNG carrier Maran Gas Mystras struck near Hormuz late Monday (TradeWinds/Ambrey) — the first LNG carrier of this wave; crew OK, ship continued sailing. TradeWinds counts 13 ships struck since Sept 29 — vs the WSJ/UKMTO count of 7 since Sept 28 (HOLD on a single count; scope unclear).
  • Carried from the morning: the Bab el-Mandeb vessel was named by Vanguard Tech as the Chrystal Sky — one explosion in the air within ~100 meters of the starboard side, no damage, voyage continuing, UAE owners/operators (per rivieramm citing Vanguard Tech). (MEDIUM — single chain)
  • One outlet reported US strikes on Iranian military targets late Friday in response to Thursday's ship attack (per shipandbunker) — but a separate report says the US has not struck Iran since Sept 1 (per thelevantfiles citing AP reporting on bomber movements). These conflict; HOLD.
  • The carrier picture: a US official told AP three carriers could be in the region by late October (per thelevantfiles). Axios reports additional Patriot batteries deployed to Saudi Arabia and Qatar, and that top cabinet members met Friday on the Iran war and Yemen (per Axios via europesays). (MEDIUM-HIGH)
  • The desk's read: the tanker war's first confirmed blood — and India, with 11 of its nationals hurt, is now on the record. Brent held ~$98 midday, but the frame the morning priced (“flows, not fear”) just got its first real counter-data point.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Attribution on any strike; a single wave count (TradeWinds 13 vs WSJ/UKMTO 7 — scope unclear); whether Bab el-Mandeb is connected to Hormuz; whether the US struck Iran on Friday (conflicting reports); the IMO evacuation-delay detail (single source).

Saudi Arabia — EVENING

JAZAN AND NAJRAN AIRPORTS CONFIRMED STRUCK

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation said Tuesday that two attacks on Monday evening struck Jazan and Najran airports — the King Abdullah bin Abdulaziz International Airport (a dual-use airport in the Red Sea city of Jazan) and Najran International Airport — injuring three people and causing material damage. “The attacks resulted in three minor injuries and material damage,” the authority said; it is assessing the damage and taking safety measures for passengers and staff (Reuters, WSJ, AP). (HIGH)
  • The Saudi statement did NOT name the attacker. The Houthis claimed the strikes late Monday (spokesman Saree: a “large number of ballistic and cruise missiles and drones”) and again warned all international airlines using Saudi airspace to halt flights — except over Mecca and Medina. (Houthi attribution: self-claim only, 80% — HOLD for independent confirmation)
  • The context: Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces pressed a major offensive to retake territory after weeks of Houthi advances, claiming Monday they had recaptured the Red Sea port of Mocha — the Houthis deny losing it (independent verification unavailable). The Saudi-led coalition says ~100 fighter jets are supporting the push, with air cover around Bab el-Mandeb (coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki). (HIGH as reported claims)
  • The aviation risk is now official-adjacent: the EU Aviation Safety Agency has warned airlines that “recurrent missile and drone attacks, together with the activation of air-defense systems, create an increased risk of misidentification of civil aircraft.” European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen: “These attacks keep destabilizing the region, igniting further violence and undermining global energy security.” (per WSJ)
  • Separate Houthi claims NOT confirmed by the Saudi acknowledgement: Monday strikes on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport and an Aramco refinery in Rabigh (AFP via The National: “could not be immediately verified”) — and a Tuesday Houthi claim of a ballistic-missile hit on Abha airport. The GACA confirmation covers Jazan and Najran only.
  • EVENING — the WSJ headlines the strikes as Houthi work: 'Yemen's Houthi Rebels Strike Two Airports in Saudi Arabia' — the kingdom said Tuesday the escalation threatens global energy markets. On the ground: AP Monday — Saudi-backed Yemeni forces retook Dhubab (just north of Bab el-Mandeb), Bab and Hadeid in a Monday offensive under Saudi air cover; witnesses saw government tanks entering the towns. Rubio: the kingdom and the US have a defense agreement, and the Saudis 'have a right to defend themselves' — no details on US coordination. Trump on the offensive: 'It will work out very well.' (HIGH / MEDIUM-HIGH)
  • One to HOLD: Israeli media reported Monday that Israel is preparing a potential attack against Iran — either coordinated with the US or independently. Single source — not run as fact.
  • The desk's read: the first official Saudi admission of strike damage on its airports in this phase — the Red Sea war's second front has landed on civilian aviation tarmac. Watch for a Riyadh follow-through.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Houthi attribution (self-claim only; Saudi did not name the attacker); the Riyadh-airport and Rabigh-refinery claims (unverified); the Abha ballistic-missile claim; whether airlines reroute.

No new US response had surfaced. The carrier calendar: the Roosevelt en route with an end-November arrival; ~9–10K troops in the movement.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

Tigray · Day 14 — EVENING

THE FALL DIDN'T END IT

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Airstrikes and artillery fire hit northern Tigray overnight into Monday morning, almost two weeks into the clashes — air attacks killed at least 17 people northwest of Mekelle, nine in Adwa and eight in Sheraro, per a humanitarian bulletin circulated among aid groups; heavy artillery was exchanged near the Tekeze River at Mai Tsebri (per Reuters). (MEDIUM-HIGH that Reuters reported it; the casualties themselves single-source)
  • Reuters could not independently confirm the reports, and no one said who launched the airstrikes. Abiy's office, the federal forces and the TPLF did not respond to requests for comment. Analysts call this the worst escalation since the 2020–2022 civil war (per Reuters).
  • TPLF chairman Debretsion Gebremichael: “We will strike the enemy where we decide, not where they dictate” (per BBC via yoopya). In Mekelle, civilians looted government offices and police headquarters as TPLF-allied fighters withdrew (per AP). The US has called on Ethiopia and Eritrea to exercise restraint (per av1tv).
  • Carried from Monday: the leaders of Egypt, Eritrea, Sudan and Somalia met at El-Alamein and agreed to coordinate military and intelligence efforts if the conflict hits their shared borders with Ethiopia (per anonymous Egyptian officials via Reuters — single source, HOLD).
  • The desk's read: Saturday answered “will Mekelle hold.” Monday answered the follow-up: the TPLF regrouped and the war moved, exactly as predicted. An unnamed-strike air campaign over Tigray is the new escalation — nobody claims it. Day 14.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Who launched the airstrikes; where the TPLF regroups; whether Eritrea re-enters.
