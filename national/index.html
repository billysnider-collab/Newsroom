Space · Starship Flight 14

CONFIRMED — FIRST ORBIT, 26 SATS UP, ONE ENGINE LOST

What we know CONFIRMED

  • SpaceX confirms: Starship lifted off Monday ~8:48 AM ET (7:48 AM CT) and reached orbit — the first time in program history. Space.com pegged orbital velocity at ~17,500 mph; NASASpaceFlight put the orbit at ~262 x 277 km, 32-degree inclination. (Reuters, USA Today, NPR/Space.com, HIGH)
  • All 26 Starlink V3 satellites deployed; Musk: 'operating nominally.' SpaceX says it made contact with every satellite over laser links, ground stations, and Starlink user terminals. (HIGH)
  • One Raptor vacuum engine shut down during ascent; the remaining five burned longer to compensate. Mission controllers ended the flight at the first scheduled decision point: deorbit burn ~2 hours in, Pacific splashdown ~2 hours 40 minutes — total ~3 hours 8 minutes vs the planned ~10 hours. (SpaceX recap, Reuters, HIGH)
  • A post-splashdown fire aboard Ship 41 in the northern Pacific was confirmed (ABC News). (MEDIUM)
  • SpaceX calls the mission a success — both primary objectives met. CFO Bret Johnsen called it the first revenue-generating Starship flight at a recent Goldman Sachs conference. (Investor's Business Daily, HIGH)
  • Musk: Flight 15 later this year will attempt to catch the ship. (MEDIUM)
  • The desk's read: the anomaly budget worked — engine-out tolerance is the reusability story. The post-splashdown fire is the footnote to watch.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • SpaceX's root cause on the engine shutdown; the Flight 15 date.
