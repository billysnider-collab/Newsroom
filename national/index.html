Iran · Day 209

ARAGHCHI NAMES THE 7-DAY PLAN — "THE CHOICE NOW RESTS WITH THE UNITED STATES"

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Reuters, Friday afternoon: Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told reporters at the UN that Tehran has given Washington a "concrete" seven-day plan — "If the necessary conditions are met, the strait can be reopened, a normal maritime passage restored within seven days. The choice now rests with the United States."
  • The price, per Araghchi and the AP readout: the US lifts its naval blockade on Iranian ports, releases Iran’s frozen funds, waives sanctions on Iranian oil sales, and observes a ceasefire that includes Lebanon. The plan builds on the June MOU — with an accelerated timetable.
  • The June MOU’s ghost: the earlier deal had a 60-day interim period and collapsed in early July when Iran resumed firing on ships. An accelerated clock does not fix the durability problem — that is what the negotiators in New York are actually arguing about.
  • The hardening: a senior Iranian official told Reuters Friday there will be NO flexibility on the nuclear program even if the US accepts the Hormuz proposal — the strait stays closed until "all of Iran’s conditions are met." The phased deal is Hormuz-for-blockade, not a nuclear deal.
  • The US read: a US official told the NY Post the talks were "positive" and "constructive"; a White House official said "Iran is desperate for a deal, President Trump holds all the cards and will make whatever decision is best for the United States."
  • Qatar is mediating — Pezeshkian hosted Qatar’s prime minister early Friday; Bloomberg reports Qatari officials carrying the messages.
  • The market heard it: Brent settled -2.1% at $104.32 Friday; WTI -2.3% at $92.41 — before any ship has moved.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether Trump accepts the 7-day clock — he has said he is in no rush, and Tehran has now given him a political-benefit argument for before the midterms (via Trita Parsi).
  • What "normal maritime passage" means in practice — escorted corridors or full normal traffic; and how the naval blockade lifts.
  • Attribution on this week’s missile fire at Saudi; the cargo-seizure bill; anything verifiable on Salalah.

Africa · the new front

TIGRAY DECLARES "FULL-BLOWN WAR" — THE FIGHT SPREADS

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The TPLF declared the conflict a "full-blown war" on September 24, as fighting spread from Tigray into Afar and Amhara — shattering the November 2022 Pretoria peace agreement that ended a war estimated to have killed ~600,000 people.
  • Wednesday’s moves: Tigrayan forces seized three federally controlled airports (Mekelle, Axum, Shire); Ethiopian Airlines has suspended all flights to Tigray.
  • Thursday/Friday: heavy artillery reported around Yallo in Afar (per AFP, via humanitarian sources); in Amhara, federal forces firing heavy weapons from a camp at Lalibela — home to medieval UNESCO rock-hewn churches; clashes in Abala and Erebti (Afar).
  • The federal army claims 272 TPLF fighters killed in North Wollo (Amhara) and 260 wounded — UNVERIFIED; AFP/Africanews could not independently confirm the figure.
  • The politics: the TPLF has allied with six other armed groups aiming to overthrow Abiy Ahmed’s government (announced Sunday); Getachew Reda (Abiy adviser) says the TPLF launched "an all-out offensive"; Tigray’s government says it is countering a federal "invasion" — the trigger is still contested.
  • NetBlocks confirmed a subnational internet disruption in northern Ethiopia Friday; panic buying emptied shelves in Tigray; analysts flag Eritrea as a possible external entrant.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Casualty counts — no independently verified figure yet; the 272 claim is federal-army sourced only.
  • The trigger — both sides’ accounts conflict; whether this is contained or spreading; Eritrea’s involvement.

Russia · refineries

PERM REFINERY HALTED — FRIDAY DRONE WAVE REACHES 1,460 KM FROM MOSCOW

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Reuters, Friday: Russia’s Perm oil refinery (LUKOIL Permnefteorgsintez, PNOS) halted operations after a Friday drone attack, per two industry sources; fire damaged pipelines, storage, and technological units.
  • Perm is Russia’s seventh-largest oil processing plant by volume — 12.6M tons processed in 2024 (252,000 bpd), yielding ~2M tons gasoline, 5.3M tons diesel, plus fuel oil and petroleum coke.
  • Same overnight wave: ~50 drones over the Rostov region; the Novoshakhtinsk refinery was damaged and forced to temporarily suspend operations, per Rostov governor Yuri Slyusar — no casualties there.
  • Perm governor Dmitry Makhonin confirmed an industrial site in the region came under attack (he did not name the plant); one man was killed in the Perm region during the wave.
  • Russia’s MOD says 592 drones were destroyed overnight; Lukoil did not reply to a request for comment.
  • A campaign continuation, not a first: the Perm plant was previously struck in April, May, July, and Aug 21 (operations suspended then too).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The extent of the damage — the sourcing is two unnamed industry sources; no independent damage assessment has surfaced.
  • Ukraine has not officially claimed responsibility for this specific strike.
  • The repair timeline and how long Perm stays down; any effect on October allocations.
