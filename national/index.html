Iran · Day 218 — MORNING

TEHRAN ANSWERS — SEVEN CONDITIONS BEFORE THE STRAIT OPENS

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Parliament speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf said Sunday the Strait of Hormuz will NOT reopen until seven Iranian conditions from the June interim agreement (the Islamabad MoU) are met — days after Tehran received Washington's reply to its diplomatic proposal (Reuters). Qalibaf: “The position of the Islamic Republic of Iran is completely clear and firm” (Nournews via Reuters). (HIGH)
  • Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi told foreign ambassadors in Tehran Sunday that the seven-day plan could reopen the strait within seven days if accepted — and warned: “If they again move towards military solutions, we are more prepared than before and will take whatever action is necessary to defend ourselves” (Reuters). (HIGH)
  • The seven-day proposal's terms (Araqchi to the AP, Sept 25): the US lifts its naval blockade, waives sanctions on Iranian oil sales, and observes a ceasefire including Lebanon; the strait opens on day six, talks begin day seven. Trump rejected it as “unacceptable” Sat Sept 26 (CNN, Sept 28). (HIGH)
  • Trump Saturday in Texas: the war ends “very quickly,” oil prices “drop like a rock” once it does; he asserted Iran has “301 percent inflation,” “no money” and “no currency,” and its military, navy and air force are “gone” (per IANS). Day 218. (HIGH on the quotes)
  • The carrier calendar: the Roosevelt is en route with an end-November arrival; ~9–10K troops in the movement. (HIGH, carried)
  • The desk's read: Tehran answered Washington's reply with a price list — seven conditions before the strait opens, plus a warning that it's more prepared for the military track. The diplomacy is now a haggle with a carrier sailing toward it.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The contents of Washington's reply; what the seven conditions actually are (not enumerated publicly); the next-round date; whether the post-midterms strike threat becomes orders.

Hormuz — MORNING

THE STRIKE TEMPO IS NOW DAILY

What we know CONFIRMED

  • UKMTO reported Sunday morning that a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz — engine-room damage, crew safe, no environmental impact; the notice did not specify when the incident occurred (Reuters via UKMTO). (HIGH)
  • Iran's IRGC, via semi-official Fars, claims its navy has hit 7 “violating” oil tankers in five days — “more than one each day,” despite Trump's claim the strait is open (Fars via IANS). Independent observer MenchOsint counted two tanker explosions on Friday. (HIGH on the UKMTO report; the Fars count is a claim)
  • The named-vessel list through Thursday: Al Funtas, Kazimah III, Al Ruwais, Mersin Prosperity, Sinbad (Splash247, UKMTO). (HIGH)
  • Flows reported at ~98% of prewar (JPM via WSJ, Friday). (HIGH)
  • MIDDAY: the IRGC navy issued a radio warning Saturday to ships planning to use the U.S.-backed route through the strait — “Don’t trust U.S. Navy and don’t use south corridor at all and don’t put your life in danger” — transmitted on a public shipping channel and reviewed by the WSJ. UKMTO has reported seven strikes on vessels around the waterway since Sept. 28. The renewed attacks may already be taking a toll: early signs that oil flows are beginning to slow, analysts say (WSJ). (HIGH on the warning and tally; the flow-slow read is analyst inference with data lagging)
  • The desk's read: Sunday's strike is the first incident of the new week — it lands straight into the Sunday-night futures open, which is how the week's oil pricing gets set. The pattern is daily and now publicly claimed by the IRGC.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Attribution for the Sunday strike; whether Fars's 7-in-5-days count is real (the agency is the claimant); the exact incident time; whether flows hold 98%.

Tigray · Day 12 — MORNING

MEKELLE FELL — THE 12-DAY FALL

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Pro-government Tigray Peace Forces and the ENDF entered the center of Mekelle on Saturday afternoon; a humanitarian source and a Mekelle resident confirmed the city is in government hands (Reuters, Sunday). (HIGH)
  • The TPLF confirmed the retreat on Facebook — the regional government “temporarily moved out of Mekelle.” The prime minister's office did not respond to a request for comment. (HIGH)
  • Alula Aba Nega Airport serving Mekelle was recaptured Saturday (dntwok; three-source Reuters reporting Saturday). (MEDIUM-HIGH)
  • The Sept 23 offensive's entire rationale was reversing federal control — losing the capital in 12 days is a rout, and the TPLF leadership is now a government-in-flight. (HIGH on the dates)
  • The desk's read: the war's question changed overnight — from “will Mekelle hold” to “does the TPLF survive as a fighting force.” Watch the retreat's direction, not the capital.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Where the TPLF regroups; whether Eritrea re-enters; an updated wounded count (MSF's 700+ is now days old).

Kyiv — MORNING

THE FOURTH DAY — MERZ ARRIVES TO A BRIDGE ON FIRE

What we know CONFIRMED

  • A Russian drone plunged into the Northern Bridge's roadway Sunday morning — the second strike on the bridge in two days, the fourth consecutive day of strikes on Kyiv's Dnipro crossings; the Southern Bridge was hit five times on Thursday and Friday. Two people injured (Klitschko, via CNN). (HIGH)
  • Klitschko says the blast ruptured a key heating main, temporarily cutting hot water to the Desnyanskyi district. Kyiv's infrastructure minister said Sunday the city will start building alternative Dnipro crossings. (HIGH)
  • Russia's Defense Ministry said Sunday it will intensify strikes on military facilities, defense-industry sites and related infrastructure in Kyiv and elsewhere — in response to a Saturday Reuters interview in which Zelensky vowed to double down on Russian refineries while not targeting civilians (Reuters via TBS News). (HIGH)
  • German Chancellor Friedrich Merz arrived in Kyiv on an unannounced visit with executives from German defense companies, pledging further aid and German-Ukrainian defense projects — to air-raid sirens. Merz: “Ukraine needs all our support in the coming weeks.” (Reuters, CNN) (HIGH)
  • MIDDAY: the package landed — German officials say Merz will announce €1 billion ($1.13 billion) in military aid plus €350 million for energy-sector repairs, and a deal for joint production of drones is due to be finalised (Reuters). Merz's delegation includes Economy Minister Katherina Reiche and executives from Siemens Energy, Hensoldt, Diehl Defence and Quantum Systems. Four air-raid alerts sounded across Kyiv on Sunday morning; Merz and Zelenskyy visited the Academy of Sciences building, where a Russian drone killed at least two people last week. (HIGH)
  • The desk's read (provisional pro-Ukraine framing): the diplomatic calendar and the bombing calendar collided on camera — Merz walked in with the defense executives while the bridge was still smoking. Berlin's commitment is now on the record against the very strikes it's meant to answer.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The ~Oct 5 checkpoint outcome (1 day out); the scale of the coming power-grid campaign; whether the alternative crossings materialize.

FZ1073 — MORNING

THE TERROR CLASSIFICATION LANDS

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The UAE Attorney-General's office stated Saturday that the co-pilot's cockpit attack on flydubai flight FZ1073 is being investigated as an attempted “terrorist operation” — the airline had initially described it as a flight-deck altercation (Airways Mag; Al Jazeera). (HIGH)
  • The suspect is identified across outlets as Hamam al-Hammami, 29, an Omani national — removed from Oman Air duties last year amid extremist-views concerns (WSJ/AP/Fox/ABC). (HIGH on identity; MEDIUM-HIGH on the prior removal — the basis rests on anonymous sources)
  • Israeli sources told CNN he probably acted alone, with no known group or government link “at the moment.” The Iran accusation died Thursday when Israel said no Iranian involvement had been identified. (MEDIUM-HIGH)
  • Netanyahu called it a “radicalized attack” with intent to crash the plane. Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohammed visited the wounded captain, Smit Machchhar, on Saturday; the GCAA says he is back in the UAE in good condition. (HIGH)
  • Flydubai's Tel Aviv suspension stands. (HIGH)
  • The desk's read: the accusation column keeps emptying while the evidence column fills — the lone-radicalization case is what's left standing, and the hiring chain is where the accountability story lives.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Motive; whether he was directed by anyone; what flydubai knew before hiring him.
