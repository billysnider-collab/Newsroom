Iran · Day 217 — MORNING

THE ROOSEVELT IS SAILING — TEHRAN STILL SILENT ON DAY 217

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The third carrier has a name and a sailing date: the USS Theodore Roosevelt left San Diego Sunday (Sept 27) on deployment, joining the USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington — with forces expected in place by end-November; up to 10,000 sailors and Marines plus an amphibious ready group are in the movement (WSJ, ISW-CTP). (HIGH)
  • The force detail: ~9,000 troops (7,000 sailors + 2,000 Marines) — the Roosevelt carrier strike group (USS Chosin) plus the USS Makin Island amphibious readiness group (USS John P. Murtha, USS Anchorage), arriving late October to November (AP). Total naval presence tops 20,000 sailors and Marines — the Navy’s first three-carrier theater since April. (HIGH)
  • Trump’s formulation: “They’ll either sign a very fair deal, or they won’t exist any longer.” (HIGH)
  • Araqchi said Friday the US steps — release frozen funds, lift oil sanctions, end the port blockade — could be completed within four or five days, clearing the way for Hormuz to reopen and final-agreement talks to begin (The Sun, MEDIUM on the Iranian framing); Iranian officials tell Reuters a deal is “very unlikely.” (HIGH on the quotes)
  • Tehran has issued no formal answer — the Friday binary expired unresolved. Day 217. (HIGH)
  • The desk’s read: the diplomacy is a holding pattern and the logistics are the policy — every headline until November reads against the Roosevelt’s position.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Tehran’s formal answer and its timing; the next-round date; the substance of any amendments; whether the post-midterms bombing threat becomes orders.

Hormuz — MORNING

THE STRIKE TEMPO IS NOW A CAMPAIGN

What we know CONFIRMED

  • At least five commercial vessels have been hit in five days — Al Funtas (Sept 28), Al Ruwais, Mersin Prosperity, Sinbad (Tuesday), Kazimah III (Thursday) — plus a projectile inside Saudi Arabia’s Yanbu port on Thursday (Splash247, UKMTO, Marisks). (HIGH)
  • The Kuwait-flagged VLCC Kazimah III was struck by an unknown projectile Thursday — the second Kuwait Oil Tanker Company VLCC hit in four days (Al Funtas, Sept 28) — and five Indian nationals were rescued Friday/Saturday by the Indian Embassy in Oman (UKMTO, Indian media). (HIGH)
  • Jaishankar says India has joined a Liberia-led 23-partner coalition to defend trade routes, and the Indian Navy’s Operation Urja Suraksha is escorting commercial vessels through the strait (Particle News, Indian media). (HIGH)
  • The flows are holding: Middle East crude exports at 17.5M b/d on a 10-day average — 98% of prewar levels (JPM via WSJ). (HIGH)
  • The desk’s read: the flows are holding at 98% while the strikes keep coming — that’s the paradox to watch. The first week the export rebound breaks, Brent stops being a premium story and becomes a supply story.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Attribution for every strike (none claimed); whether flows can hold 98% if the tempo continues; whether the coalition becomes an escort regime.

  • On the other ledger: Ukraine confirmed the first combat use of its FP-7 domestic ballistic missile on Oct 2 and struck the Transneft Samara oil tank farm — “the largest tank farm in Europe” — overnight Oct 1–2 (OSINT daily brief, Ukrainian General Staff). (MEDIUM-HIGH)
  • The NYT published an assessment that Russia is planning a three-stage campaign targeting nuclear-plant substations, hydroelectric facilities, and water infrastructure ahead of winter. (MEDIUM-HIGH — assessment, not findings)
  • The desk’s read (provisional pro-Ukraine framing): Moscow struck a second Kyiv bridge in three days while Kyiv put its first domestic ballistic missile into combat — both sides are opening new ladders in the same week.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether the ~Oct 5 checkpoint produces anything (2 days out); the scale of the coming power-grid campaign.

Energy · the diesel deal — MORNING

RESOLVED — THE G7 RELEASED, THE BAN WAS “NEVER ON THE TABLE”

What we know CONFIRMED

  • After Thursday’s ultimatum (release 120M barrels of diesel or face a US export ban), Macron convened an emergency G7 videoconference Friday. The outcome: G7 leaders agreed a coordinated release of up to 100 million barrels of diesel and crude over four months, supervised by the IEA, with a “frontloaded substantial diesel release” in the first ~20 days — and a pledge of no export restrictions on energy between G7 members (CNN, Le Monde, USA Today, Macron). (HIGH)
  • Trump declared “Europe has just agreed to release a massive amount of their heavily stocked Diesel Oil,” then said the US diesel export ban was “never really on the table” (Truth Social, The Times). (HIGH)
  • AAA: regular gas averaged $4.40/gal Friday, down 9+ cents w/w (NY Post). Treasury’s Bessent noted the US supplied nearly half of March’s 400M-barrel release, with the latest 40M tranche unlocked Tuesday. (HIGH)
  • The desk’s read: the ban threat worked as leverage and then vanished — Europe called the bluff by delivering a smaller, oil-heavy version of the ask. The frame gap (Trump’s “massive diesel” vs the actual 100M-barrel mixed release) is where the story lives.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The actual diesel volume in the frontloaded tranche (unspecified); whether releases begin “immediately” as claimed.

FZ1073 — MIDDAY

THE UAE CALLS IT TERRORISM — THE CO-PILOT USED THE COCKPIT CRASH AXE

What we know CONFIRMED

  • UAE Attorney-General Hamad Saif Al Shamsi said Saturday, via state agency WAM, that the co-pilot of flydubai flight FZ1073 ‘attacked the captain inside the flight deck using a crash ax and attempted to take control of the aircraft’s flight controls’ — the first formal description by Emirati authorities of the Sept 30 Dubai-to-Tel Aviv incident as an attempted ‘terrorist’ attack. (HIGH on the on-record statement)
  • The weapon explains the mechanics: crash axes are kept in the cockpit for emergencies, and UAE aviation rules require one aboard — the co-pilot likely did not need to smuggle a weapon onto the 737. (WSJ, HIGH)
  • Press reports identify the co-pilot as 29-year-old Omani national Hamam al-Hammami — WSJ reported Friday that Oman had barred him from flying over concerns he had adopted extremist views; a Reuters source Saturday gave the name as ‘Hammam al-Hammami’. The UAE prosecutor’s statement did not name him. (MEDIUM-HIGH; press-sourced, spelling varies across outlets)
  • Israeli investigators will take part in the UAE-led investigation, an Israeli official told Reuters; PM Netanyahu said Friday Israel is examining whether the co-pilot acted under anyone’s direction, warning anyone involved would pay a ‘heavy price.’ Israeli officials told Reuters earlier that no Iranian involvement has been identified so far. (HIGH)
  • The captain, Smit Machchhar, remains in hospital; the attacker was detained in Saudi Arabia after the emergency landing at Tabuk. (HIGH)

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The motive — investigators are still establishing ‘the full circumstances, motives, and any links’ (Al Shamsi).
  • Whether he was a ‘lone wolf or has any connection with any extremist groups’ (Al Shamsi) — the central open question.
  • The vetting trail: how Hamam al-Hammami was hired by flydubai after Oman barred him from flying.

MEKELLE IN THE CROSSHAIRS

What we know CONFIRMED

(HIGH on the shutdown; MEDIUM on the advance claim)

  • Ethiopia expelled an Egyptian diplomat (48h); the AU called for maximum restraint; the US offered to facilitate mediation. MSF: 700+ wounded treated at hospitals across Tigray. (HIGH)
  • New Saturday: the pro-government Tigray Peace Forces militia claimed on X late Friday that it had retaken Mekelle’s Alula Aba Nega Airport and was ‘clearing the city’ — Reuters reported the claim but could not verify it; the prime minister’s office did not respond and the TPLF could not be reached. A diplomatic source told Reuters some senior TPLF leaders had already left Mekelle. (MEDIUM on the claim; unverified by independent reporting)
  • The desk’s read: when embassies close and drones hit the capital’s defence ministry, the war is past the point where statements matter — watch the A2 corridor.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether Eritrean forces re-enter combat; the Alamata civilian claim; whether Mekelle falls.

Yemen — MORNING

MISSILE DEBRIS OVER SAUDI CITIES; A COUNTEROFFENSIVE PLANNED

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Saudi Civil Defence said Houthi ballistic-missile debris fell in the Asir region on Saturday — one resident injured, a prayer hall damaged in Ahad Rafidah; a separate projectile fell on a school in Najran (material damage). The Houthis claim 94 Saudi strikes in 24 hours and 1,350 since the fighting renewed (unverified). (HIGH on the debris impacts; MEDIUM on the Axios planning)
  • Axios reports Saudi Arabia and Yemen’s government are planning a major Red Sea coast counteroffensive aimed at pushing the Houthis away from Bab al-Mandeb — Saudi leadership approved the plans in recent days; Yemeni government forces would lead the ground operation. The US declined direct airstrikes — intel and targeting support only. (MEDIUM-HIGH)
  • The WFP says hunger is accelerating to record levels: in government-controlled areas (~10M people), three-quarters of households are skipping meals. (HIGH)
  • The desk’s read: the 2022 truce is gone on paper and in the air — the question now is whether the Red Sea coast becomes the ground war’s second front.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The operation’s launch date; the Houthi strike claims (unverified).
