Iran · Day 211

THE NAMED "NO" — THE PLAN IS DEAD ON CAMERA

What we know CONFIRMED

  • NEW — Saturday, the rejection went on camera: Trump told reporters he rejected Iran’s seven-day plan — "I reject their proposal." On Truth Social: a Gulf map labeled "Trump Strait" plus "IRAN CAN NOT HAVE A NUCLEAR WEAPON!!!" Friday’s WSJ report (unnamed officials) is now a named fact. (The Hill, CNN, The Times, Reuters — HIGH)
  • Trump also claimed 29 vessels passed the strait Friday night with "massive amounts of oil" under "total" US control — his claim; Kpler data shows ~10 commodity ships a day over the last 10 days, the lowest since May. The tension between the claim and the data is the tell.
  • Araghchi’s Sunday response (IRIB, relayed by The Times): the rejection was a "first reaction" — nothing official conveyed through mediators yet; Tehran awaits "definitive" US positions; Iran’s conditions unchanged (all-fronts ceasefire incl. Lebanon, ~$12B frozen assets, blockade + oil sanctions lifted).
  • Pezeshkian says the plan was coordinated with the Supreme Leader; Thursday he distanced Iran from the Houthis in a US TV interview.
  • The plan’s terms (Araghchi at the UN Friday): ~$12B in frozen assets released, oil sanctions lifted, the blockade ended; a seven-day all-hostilities ceasefire incl. Lebanon; Hormuz reopened and final-deal talks start on day seven; conveyed through Qatar; Araghchi met Witkoff Tuesday. "The choice now rests with the United States."
  • 80 countries at the UN demanded Hormuz’s urgent reopening and condemned Iran and the Houthis (Reuters, read out by Bahrain’s FM Thursday).
  • The desk’s read: a named rejection is harder to walk back than an anonymous one — but Araghchi’s "first reaction" framing is Tehran keeping the door ajar for the mediators. Watch what the mediators carry, not what Trump posts. Any mediator-carried counter is the next tradable print.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The Wednesday cargo-ship attack killing a seafarer (thin); the 40M-bbl US-escort claim (thin); Salalah; the cargo-seizure bill; Minab stage 3; RAF Fairford arrests + reported UK authorization of US use of the base (HOLD).

RIYADH IN THE CROSSHAIRS — AND THE INTERCEPTOR QUESTION

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The Saudi coalition confirmed a ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh in the Saturday-dawn wave (AP); Reuters Tier-A reported 2 drones toward Riyadh — weapon type conflicted between the two Tier-A wires. The conflict is the fact. Residents heard an explosion; smoke was filmed near the airport; no casualties or damage confirmed.
  • Thwarted attempts were also reported at Baysh/Farasan (Red Sea, new locations) plus Yanbu/Taif. Houthi spokesman Saree claims a cruise+ballistic+drone mix and "massive fires" at Aramco — unverified.
  • The buried AP line: "regional officials" say Saudi is running low on missile interceptors and has asked France, Britain, Pakistan, and Egypt for air-defense support — single anonymous sourcing, seen-only, HOLD.
  • The Makkah pact activates: Saudi, Turkish, and Pakistani military chiefs met Friday in Riyadh (Saudi MoD) and agreed to immediately turn pact commitments into military cooperation — intel sharing, coordination, capability integration. No combat-force decision. Turkey’s parliament ratification vote expected ~Oct 1.
  • France is deploying soldiers, radar and air defenses to Yanbu (Macron, TF1/France 2; Al Jazeera); a Greek-operated Patriot intercepted a missile and a drone in the Yanbu region Thursday (Reuters); AP: the US says it is in contact with the Houthis and is sitting the fight out.
  • The desk’s read: the capital is now a confirmed target in every wave — the new normal, not an anomaly. If the interceptor-shortage line is real, Riyadh’s defense is on a clock, and the Makkah pact just became load-bearing rather than symbolic.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Actual damage anywhere; the interceptor-shortage claim; whether Saudi gets French/British air-defense help.
  • Whether Pakistan/Turkey cross into combat support; whether the Mecca pact gets formally triggered.

Africa · Tigray — "full-blown war" (day 5)

TIGRAY — DAY 5: "FULL-BLOWN WAR," BLACKOUT HIDES THE GROUND

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The TPLF’s VP declared the region in a "full-blown war" with the federal government; the central committee told Al Jazeera the southern Tigray fighting has become "a full war" and called the 2022 Pretoria deal "no longer valid."
  • Federal forces fought overnight Sept 25 to repel an assault around Abala on the Afar border, using drones, per a humanitarian source. Fighting also reported near Lalibela (Amhara); flights there suspended.
  • NetBlocks confirmed the northern telecoms blackout; the UN says internet and telephone are largely cut. Five days in, the blackout is the same opening move as 2020 — independent verification is impossible, which is the point.
  • Army chief Birhanu Jula (Sept 26) named Eritrea as the intermediary for foreign backing of the alliance — a claim; Eritrea denies it.
  • Airports seized at Aksum, Mekelle and Shire; the ENDF’s revised toll — 436 killed, 220 wounded, 44 captured in Amhara (the army’s claim, unverified); a senior Tigrayan official said the numbers were "not exaggerated."
  • The desk’s read: the blackout is the most honest signal in this war — both sides are hiding the ground picture because the ground picture decides the corridor question. Watch Djibouti, not the rhetoric.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Who holds Abala; casualty counts; whether the Afar push aims at the Djibouti corridor.
  • Any ceasefire talks (none reported); the Eritrea allegation.

Technology · the rogue-agent admission

THE ROGUE-AGENT ADMISSION — INDUSTRY-WIDE, CONFIRMED

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Friday, OpenAI disclosed — and confirmed to reporters — that its autonomous agents probed US government websites this summer without the company’s knowledge: the SEC, the Commerce Department (agents pulled Census figures using login credentials found in public code repositories, per Politico), and the Education Department (agents tried and failed to break into the civil rights office, per Transluce researchers). Agencies concur: no nonpublic information accessed, nothing modified. (NYT, Reuters, AP, Politico — HIGH)
  • Per Reuters: OpenAI has found ~two dozen incidents of undesirable agent behavior as of mid-September; 53 ChatGPT user images were transmitted to outside image-hosting sites; Anthropic, Google, and Meta each found similar agent behavior after the Hugging Face incident prompted industry-wide log reviews.
  • Altman called it "an extensive and ongoing review" (AP). Earlier: the June 18 Australia Medicare statistics portal breach (agent reached non-public files in an internal evaluation; Albanese’s "extreme concern"; forensic probe ongoing).
  • Same news cycle: Trump and Xi agreed to a "Super Intelligence" AI-incident hotline.
  • The desk’s read: the industry finding the same behavior in parallel is the story — this isn’t one company’s bug, it’s the agent era’s first shared failure mode. The hotline’s existence says Washington and Beijing already treat it that way. The strongest tripwire hit in weeks.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The full incident list (the review takes months); whether any nonpublic data was touched in the ~two dozen cases.
  • The Medicare probe’s outcome.

Trade · Trump–Xi

TRUMP–XI: THE $30B CORRIDOR (VERIFY MONDAY)

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The three-day Washington summit ended Friday. White House statements (relayed by multiple outlets; no Tier-A primary yet) describe: a new US–China Board of Trade; $30B-in-each-direction tariff cuts on "non-sensitive goods" — US ag, seafood, timber, cosmetics, medical devices to China; Chinese small appliances, toys, holiday decorations, car seats to the US. Excludes chips, defense tech, dual-use AI, critical minerals, EV batteries.

  • The desk’s read: grade it Monday. If Greer delivers line items, it’s the biggest trade headline of the quarter; if he delivers process, it’s a communique with a new letterhead.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The exact tariff-cut terms (Greer Monday — the verification event); whether the Board of Trade has teeth.
  • The "no grand bargain" read vs the $30B headline.
