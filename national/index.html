Pipeline attack · Trump's call

Trump: Iran "probably" behind the Saudi pipeline attack

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Trump, in Dublin on Saturday: "I think they are, probably they are," when asked whether Iran was behind Thursday's drone attack on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline (Reuters, CNN).
  • Saudi foreign ministry confirmed Friday: the pipeline in the Riyadh and Madinah regions was hit Thursday by several drones "coming from Iraq" — injuries and some damage; the energy ministry shut it "as a precautionary measure" (CNN).
  • Two US officials told CNN pump stations next to the pipeline were hit Thursday; satellite imagery Friday showed extensive fire damage at one pumping station (CNN).
  • The Islamic Resistance in Iraq — the main Iran-backed militia umbrella — denied responsibility (CNN).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Who ordered the attack: Riyadh says the drones came from Iraq; the accused militia umbrella denies it; Trump points at Iran; Le Monde attributes the strike to the Houthis. These accounts cannot all be true.
  • The extent of damage to the pipeline itself and any reopening timeline.
  • What, if anything, the Houthi call to Washington committed to — Trump says they "would much prefer not having us involved" (Reuters).

Washington · Riyadh

The ask Trump said no to — twice

What we know CONFIRMED

  • MBS called Trump twice on Thursday urging direct US strikes against the Houthis as they advanced on the Red Sea coast (Axios via Reuters; CNN source). Trump declined.
  • Washington told Riyadh it would not take direct military action "at this time" and would limit support to intelligence-sharing and targeting help (CNN). CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper traveled to Saudi Arabia Thursday for urgent coordination talks.
  • The White House to CNN: the US is "focused on protecting our core national security interests — such as ensuring freedom of navigation in the Red Sea — while empowering our regional partners to take the lead."

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The exact content of the two calls — both accounts rest on unnamed US officials; neither the White House nor the Saudi embassy confirmed the strike request on the record.
  • What "intelligence sharing and targeting help" covers in practice — the scope is undefined.

Yemen · Red Sea

Mokha and Mayun fall — the Houthis sit on the strait

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Houthi forces seized the port city of Mokha and Perim Island (Mayun), which sits inside the Bab al-Mandeb Strait and divides it into two shipping channels (CNN, Reuters, Wall Street Journal).
  • The fall of Mokha came with little resistance, triggering a blame game among US allies — one regional source called it "a f***-up of epic proportions" (CNN).
  • The Houthis declared "a blockade of Saudi Arabia" on July 20 and have since struck the Jizan refinery and a Saudi tanker off Yanbu (Le Monde). They say they are blocking "only Saudi ships" — a claim Trump echoed in Dublin.
  • Saudi crude production fell roughly 1.9 million barrels a day to 6.24 million in August, down 23% from July (OPEC data via WSJ).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether the Houthis push from Mokha toward Aden, where the internationally recognized Yemeni government is based.
  • How long Riyadh can sustain the current tempo without the direct US help it just asked for.

AI misuse · first documented case

Anthropic: a Yemen cell ran Claude like a missile engineering team

What we know CONFIRMED

  • In a report released Thursday, Anthropic said a cell of actors in northern Yemen — Houthi-held territory — used Claude across three weapons programs: a guided rocket, a planned ballistic missile with a stated range above 2,000 km, and an "R2000" variant with a claimed hypersonic glide vehicle.
  • The actors divided Claude instances into coding, research, and review roles. A guided-rocket test appeared to fail; they returned to Claude within hours to troubleshoot — which is how the activity was detected (AP, Reuters).
  • Anthropic banned the accounts, shared findings with government and industry partners, and says it has no evidence an operational weapon was fielded (AP).
  • The same report covered likely Russia-based actors building autonomous FPV drone-swarm software and a Russia-based procurement manager sourcing dual-use components via China and Hong Kong (Reuters).
  • Houthi political-bureau member Hazam al-Assad called the implication "unreasonable and illogical" (AP).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether the actors were Houthis — Anthropic did not identify them; the inference is geographic.
  • The 2,000 km range and hypersonic-glide claims — both unverified, from the users themselves.

Hormuz · oil

Brent $104.61 into the weekend — Monday's Salalah meeting is the fork

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Brent settled Friday at $104.61 a barrel, down 2.8% on the day but up 8.7% on the week; WTI settled at $100.05, up 9.4% on the week (Wall Street Journal).
  • The pullback came on Financial Times reporting (via Barron's) that Gulf foreign ministers will meet Iran's counterpart in Salalah, Oman, on Monday to discuss a temporary Hormuz shipping arrangement — shaped by weeks of Oman-Iran bilateral talks (SeaNews).
  • UK Maritime Trade Operations said Thursday four unidentified projectiles struck two vessels near Khasab, Oman, causing a fire on one (WSJ).
  • US Central Command said Wednesday American forces destroyed 10 Iranian tankers over the past week; the US Energy Information Administration raised its oil price forecasts (Reuters).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Who attends Monday and whether anything is signed — Press TV also points to a Monday meeting in Oman, consistent on timing, thin on detail.
  • Whether a credible arrangement drains the risk premium — or failure refills it.

The Fed week

CPI holds 3.4% — markets price an 85% hike

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The FOMC meets Tuesday and Wednesday, September 15–16, with markets pricing roughly an 85% chance of a quarter-point rate hike after Friday's August CPI (CME FedWatch via Barron's).
  • August headline CPI rose 0.4% on the month, holding annual inflation at 3.4%; core CPI rose 0.3% on the month and 2.4% year-on-year, with core services excluding housing up 0.5% — the strongest since January (Barron's, via BLS).
  • Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said last month the Fed may need to act if underlying inflation is not moving toward 2% "clearly and at sufficient speed" (Reuters). The ECB raised rates Thursday, citing Iran-war energy inflation.
  • Vice President JD Vance said Thursday the Fed should lower rates, not raise them (Barron's).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The decision itself, and the vote split — July's meeting had three dissents favoring a hike, the largest one-way dissent bloc since 2016.

Trade · Canada

The clock runs: Sept 15 list, Sept 29 bans

What we know CONFIRMED

  • No new talks reported since Tuesday's proclamations. The standing deadlines: September 15, the revised 50% tariff product list takes effect (cement and road salt come off; ATVs, boats, and more cheese lines go on); September 29, import prohibitions begin on Canadian dairy, alcohol, and motorcycles (WSJ, The Times, Mondaq).
  • The measures retaliate for Canada's September 8 retaliatory tariffs, which answered the August 22 US Section 338 tariffs on $20 billion of Canadian goods (Mondaq, Avalara).
  • Canada's trade minister Dominic LeBlanc said Ottawa is "assessing" and will engage "in good faith" when the US is ready (WSJ). Prime Minister Carney released a video message: "We have everything we need to pivot and prosper" (The Times).
  • Trump has additionally threatened to bar Canadian firms from government procurement and raise auto tariffs to 50% on January 1, 2027 (Mondaq).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether any back-channel talks before Tuesday produce a delay — a senior administration official told the WSJ conversations "in the coming days" are possible.

Miami · NTSB

  • Aircraft: 32-year-old 767-300 (21 Air-operated Amazon Prime Air Flight 7598); overrun ~1,300 ft; struck a 7-person cleaning van.
  • 5 killed, 5 injured (NTSB Chair Homendy, USA Today).

  • Cause — NTSB explicitly refused to speculate.
  • Which pilot raised the speed concern — not disclosed.
  • The promised full CVR readout — still the next data point.
