Iran · Day 210

THE SEVEN-DAY PLAN — REJECTED ON THE RECORD

What we know CONFIRMED

  • NEW — the named “no”: Saturday afternoon, President Trump told reporters at the White House — before departing for Tennessee — “I reject their proposal. ... I rejected their deal.” “That deal would not be acceptable,” he said. “I like making a deal too.” On Iran’s motive: “They want to make a deal where they open the strait immediately because they’re losing so badly.” “We’re winning tremendously.” (Reuters — Trevor Hunnicutt, Parisa Hafezi, Menna AlaaElDin; NPR — HIGH)
  • The WSJ’s Friday leak now has a name behind it — and WSJ adds that Trump is privately skeptical Iran would meet his demands and told his staff he sees a renewed bombing campaign as likely after the November midterms (unnamed US officials — MEDIUM). Earlier, a US official had said discussions through mediators were “positive and constructive” (Reuters).
  • Trump also claimed “a tremendous amount of oil” flowed through Hormuz the previous night along with 29 vessels (Seoul Economic Daily — the traffic count is Trump’s claim: MEDIUM). President Masoud Pezeshkian stressed Saturday the proposal had been coordinated with Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei.
  • The plan itself (Araghchi, Friday at the UN, on the record): ~$12B in frozen Iranian assets released, oil sanctions lifted, the US naval blockade ended (June 17 Islamabad MoU, accelerated); all hostilities cease for seven days including Lebanon; once US steps are done (~4–5 days), Hormuz reopens and final-deal talks start on day seven. Conveyed through Qatar; Araghchi met Witkoff Tuesday. “The choice now rests with the United States.” (NYT on the contents)
  • Axios reported Trump asked Xi to cut off Iran support; Trump warned Xi against supporting Iran and received assurances China would not do so, per US ambassador David Perdue (MEDIUM); Xi urged a return to the June framework (Xinhua).
  • The market backdrop: midday Friday Brent dropped below $98 and stocks jumped (S&P +0.51%, Dow +479); the WSJ rejection report bounced oil back to ~$104.32 by the NY close. Trump’s UNGA threat — “annihilate the Islamic Republic” — now reads as the backdrop, not an aside.
  • 80 countries at the UN demanded Hormuz’s urgent reopening and condemned Iran and the Houthis for attacks and trade disruption (Reuters, read out by Bahrain’s FM Thursday).
  • The desk’s read: an unnamed-officials “no” was deniable and reversible; a named, on-camera “no” with a midterms-bombing kicker is a posture. Tehran wanted the world to see it said yes so the “no” sticks to Washington — now the “no” has a face, a microphone, and a timeline (after November). Trade Monday’s oil open, not the leak.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Why Trump rejected it — terms? timing? post-midterm leverage? (the WSJ bombing-campaign line is unnamed-officials: MEDIUM).
  • Whether the rejection is final or a bargaining floor — Iran has not answered publicly since Trump spoke.
  • The reported Wednesday cargo-ship attack in Hormuz that killed a seafarer (thinly sourced); claims of ~40M bbl transiting under US escort (thinly sourced); Salalah; the cargo-seizure bill; Minab stage 3.

Africa · Tigray — "a full war" (day 4)

TIGRAY — DAY 4: THE TOLL CLIMBS, ABALA UNDER DRONE FIRE

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The Sept 23 offensive by the seven-group Ethiopian Peoples’ Forces Alliance for Survival (TPLF/TDF plus Fano, OLA, Afar and others) has widened across three regions in four days. Airports seized at Aksum, Mekelle and Shire; the Afar town of Abala captured; a TDF official called it “full war” — the 2022 Pretoria deal is shattered. (Al Jazeera, CNN, Bloomberg, France 24)
  • NEW — the army’s toll, revised upward Friday: the ENDF says 436 fighters killed, 220 wounded and 44 captured in Amhara — the army’s claim, unverified — but a senior Tigrayan official said the numbers were “not exaggerated,” a rare admission of the scale (reported Friday).
  • NEW — heavy fighting overnight around Abala, the Afar border town: federal drones helped repel an attempt to storm the town (humanitarian source).
  • NEW — the army chief speaks: ENDF Chief of General Staff Field Marshal Birhanu Jula, speaking Saturday, warned of a “foreign-backed insurgency” and accused Eritrea of acting as the intermediary supplying the coalition.
  • The Tigray Army described the federal assault as a “full-scale offensive” (infantry, mechanised units, drones) and said its forces launched a “defensive war” (Al Jazeera). TPLF spokesman Michael Asgedom told AP the Pretoria accord is “no longer valid.”
  • The AU said the airport seizures were inconsistent with the Pretoria deal, urged de-escalation, and asked former Nigerian president Olusegun Obasanjo — who mediated the 2022 deal — for help (CNN).
  • NetBlocks confirmed a subnational internet disruption in the north; Reuters reports the Tigray side itself ordered the blackout to degrade government drone targeting. AFP: Mekelle residents are stockpiling essentials, fearing banks, power and communications could be cut again.
  • Ethiopia accused Eritrea of financing the alliance; Eritrea denied it. Israel warned citizens off Tigray; Ethiopian Airlines suspended Mekelle/Axum/Shire flights.
  • The desk’s read: the Tigray-ordered blackout is the tell — they learned from 2020 that drones own the skies, so they’re blinding the grid preemptively. A casualty toll neither side disputes and a “no longer valid” accord mean this is a planned offensive. Daily line until trajectory is clear.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Casualty counts on either side — the ENDF’s revised toll is unconfirmed and the TDF has not published its own; the trigger; whether Abala marks a push toward Djibouti’s corridor.

Red Sea · the second front

YANBU IN THE CROSSHAIRS — FRANCE SENDS TROOPS AND AIR DEFENSES

What we know CONFIRMED

  • NEW — France is deploying soldiers, radar systems and air defenses to guard Yanbu: President Macron announced it in TF1 and France 2 interviews (Al Jazeera, Friday). The stated aim: protect the site, not draw France into the conflict. France joins the UK, Italy, Greece and other European states signaling support for Riyadh.
  • Thursday’s wave: the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted six ballistic missiles aimed at Taif and Yanbu; the Houthis claimed strikes on “a sensitive target” in Riyadh and Aramco facilities in Yanbu — no Saudi confirmation of the Riyadh claim. A Greek-operated Patriot in Saudi Arabia intercepted a ballistic missile and a drone in the wider Yanbu region (Reuters).
  • NEW — Saturday ~4 a.m. Saudi time: the coalition said it intercepted missiles toward Khamis Mushait and drones toward Riyadh (Reuters/Arab News); the Houthis had not publicly claimed those launches; no verified damage or casualties.
  • Saudi Civil Defense issued emergency warnings across several areas including Mecca; the danger was later declared passed. Yemen’s recognized-government VP used UNGA to press world leaders not to trust Houthi assurances.
  • NEW — the pact moves: the foreign ministers of Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Türkiye met Thursday and agreed to arrange an urgent meeting of their military chiefs under the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement — which treats an attack on one as an attack on all. The pact has not been formally triggered.
  • A Khamenei adviser (Mojtaba Khamenei, via AFP/Fars) warned Iran could expand the war to the Indian Ocean if the US or Israel attacks again. AP: the US says it has been in contact with the Houthis and indicated it was sitting this fight out.
  • Industry sources say Saudi Arabia is building up crude pumping through the East-West pipeline — Day 5 of the reported restart (shut Sept 11 after drone attacks Riyadh blames on Iraqi militia); NO confirmed Yanbu loadings yet.
  • The desk’s read: the Houthis aren’t trying to sink the fleet — they’re trying to make Yanbu uninsurable. France putting troops and radars on the ground says the insurers’ math is the theater now. If loadings can’t resume, the pipeline restart is a headline, not a barrel.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Damage or casualties at Taif, Yanbu, Khamis Mushait or Riyadh; the France deployment’s size and timeline; whether the Mecca pact gets formally triggered.

Russia · overnight

ILSKY ON FIRE, PERM HALTED — THE REFINERY WAR GETS A SHUTDOWN

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Overnight Sept 25–26, a Ukrainian drone wave struck Krasnodar region, Russia. Ukraine’s General Staff confirmed the strike was carried out by the 1st Separate Center and the 414th Brigade (“Madyar’s Birds”), claimed under UN Charter Article 51. (Ukraine General Staff; Reuters wire)
  • The Ilsky oil refinery in Seversky district (about 50 km from Krasnodar, ~6.6M tons a year) caught fire. (Ukraine General Staff; Krasnodar governor)
  • Port Kavkaz, the Kerch Strait grain and LPG terminal, was hit: 3 people injured, a service vessel and the pier complex damaged. (Krasnodar regional authorities, via Reuters-byline reporting)
  • Governor Veniamin Kondratyev: 3 killed and 2 wounded in Seversky district; a residential building damaged by drone debris; about 26,000 residents without power. (Governor’s Telegram; picked up by the AP wire — Tier-A)
  • NEW — Perm confirmed: Reuters (two industry sources) reports the Perm oil refinery — Russia’s seventh-largest — HALTED operations after Friday’s drone attack. Fire damaged pipelines, storage and technological units. The plant (Lukoil-Permnefteorgsintez) processed 12.6M tons / 252K bpd in 2024 — 2M tons of gasoline, 5.3M tons of diesel. Governor Dmitry Makhonin confirmed an industrial site was hit Friday (did not name the plant); Lukoil declined comment. The plant sits ~1,460 km northeast of Moscow.
  • Context: Ilsky was hit before on June 2 and August 8, 2026 (the August strike injured 5); Perm was last hit August 21.
  • The desk’s read: for months the refinery war was about burning oil; this week it attached a casualty count AND a confirmed shutdown — two refineries in two days.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Russia’s MoD claims 645 drones were downed overnight — single-source claim, attributed as “Russia claims” only.
  • Afipsky refinery possibly hit — UNCONFIRMED. No claim on the Perm repair timeline.
