Pipeline attack · Iraq responds

Baghdad sacks the Maysan commander; Trump points at Iran

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Iraq's prime minister's office said early Sunday that the commander running operations in Maysan Governorate was dismissed after investigators confirmed the pipeline drones were launched from within that southern Iraqi province (reported via Reuters; the attribution is second-hand).
  • Trump, in Dublin on Saturday: Iran was "probably" behind Thursday's drone attack — "I think they are, probably they are" (Reuters, CNN).
  • Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry confirmed Friday the East-West pipeline in the Riyadh and Madinah regions was hit Thursday by several drones "coming from Iraq" — injuries and some damage; the energy ministry shut it "as a precautionary measure" (CNN).
  • Two US officials told CNN pump stations next to the pipeline were hit Thursday; satellite imagery Friday showed extensive fire damage at one pumping station (CNN).
  • Saudi Civil Defence said Saturday a Houthi-fired projectile injured two people in Jazan and damaged buildings including a mosque (Reuters).
  • Iraq closed a number of its borders Saturday as a precaution and opened an investigation. The Islamic Resistance in Iraq — the main Iran-backed militia umbrella — denied responsibility (CNN).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Who ordered the attack: Baghdad says the drones were launched from Maysan; Riyadh says they came "from Iraq"; the accused militia umbrella denies it; Trump points at Iran. These accounts are not all consistent.
  • The extent of damage to the pipeline itself and any reopening timeline — the workaround moving 4-5M bpd to Yanbu stays shut.

AI misuse · first documented case

Anthropic: a Yemen cell ran Claude like a missile engineering team

What we know CONFIRMED

  • In a report released Thursday, Anthropic said a cell of actors in northern Yemen — Houthi-held territory — used Claude across three weapons programs: a guided rocket, a planned ballistic missile with a stated range above 2,000 km, and an "R2000" variant with a claimed hypersonic glide vehicle.
  • The actors divided Claude instances into coding, research, and review roles. A guided-rocket test appeared to fail; they returned to Claude within hours to troubleshoot — which is how the activity was detected (AP, Reuters).
  • Anthropic banned the accounts, shared findings with government and industry partners, and says it has no evidence an operational weapon was fielded (AP).
  • The same report covered likely Russia-based actors building autonomous FPV drone-swarm software and a Russia-based procurement manager sourcing dual-use components via China and Hong Kong (Reuters).
  • Houthi political-bureau member Hazam al-Assad called the implication "unreasonable and illogical" (AP).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether the actors were Houthis — Anthropic did not identify them; the inference is geographic.
  • The 2,000 km range and hypersonic-glide claims — both unverified, from the users themselves.

Hormuz · Monday

Salalah Monday: two contradictory pre-briefs

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Gulf foreign ministers meet Iran's foreign minister Monday on a temporary Hormuz shipping arrangement; the meeting was called at Oman's initiative (per a senior Iranian official).
  • The same senior Iranian official told Reuters Saturday the meeting is "not expected to yield a signed agreement" — Iran still wants fees from ships using the strait, which Oman rejects; Bahrain will not attend (Reuters).
  • Separately, President Pezeshkian told India Today the meeting is in Muscat and will "sign an agreement establishing a joint route" — city and outcome both disputed (India Today).
  • Iran's foreign ministry says eight Gulf-bordering states plus Iraq are expected; Saudi participation is only via anonymous sources (WSJ/FT). Oman has not officially announced the meeting.
  • Relief valve: Iran granted a number of Iraqi oil tankers special permission to transit Hormuz after Baghdad's requests, per IRNA Saturday — confirmed by Iraqi President Nizar Amedi, who called the situation "complicated." Iraq draws roughly 90% of government revenue from oil.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether anything is signed Monday — Pezeshkian says a joint route gets signed; a senior Iranian official told Reuters Saturday no signed deal should be expected; Tasnim says the understanding provides no immediate reopening and depends on US conditions. Contradictory Iranian signals; Monday settles which is real.
  • The city (Salalah vs Muscat), the attendee list (Oman hasn't officially announced), and whether a credible arrangement drains the risk premium — or failure refills it.

The Fed week

Wednesday's decision is ~85–92% priced — the statement is the trade

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The FOMC meets Tuesday and Wednesday, September 15–16; futures now price ~85–92% odds of a 25bp hike to 3.75–4.00% at Wednesday's decision (Reuters ~85%, WSJ 87%, Barron's 92%), up from ~70% before Friday's CPI.
  • August CPI: headline 0.4% on the month, holding annual inflation at 3.4%; core 0.3% on the month and 2.4% year-on-year, with core services excluding housing up 0.5% — the strongest since January (BLS via Barron's).
  • The bond market has already tightened: 10-year above 4.9% Thursday (highest since 2023); 30-year above 5.38% (nearly a two-decade high); 2-year near 4.6% (14-month high) (Reuters). UBS now expects hikes in both September and December.
  • Deutsche Bank's Luzzetti: officials may be loath to surprise markets dovish when a hike is ~90% priced — a credibility concern after the July hold sent long-end yields soaring.
  • The ECB raised Thursday citing Iran-war energy inflation. Fed blackout is in effect; no Fed signals until the statement.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The decision itself and the vote split — 85–92% is odds, not a decision. July's meeting had three dissents favoring a hike, the largest one-way dissent bloc since 2016.
  • Whether Warsh treats the wireless-services distortion as noise — the statement language is the trade: one hike vs the start of a cycle.
