Iran · Day 212

TALKS “THIS WEEK” — THE CALENDAR IS THE STORY

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Sunday, Trump told Axios he expects more Iran talks “this week,” possibly as soon as Monday: “They want to make a deal, but it is not the deal that I want to make… what we would have maybe agreed to a year ago. They overplayed their hand.” On resuming strikes: “I am always thinking about it.” (HIGH on quotes)
  • The named 7-day rejection stands; Waltz on CNN: it was rejected because Iran wanted sanctions lifted and assets released “up front.”
  • Araghchi late Sunday on X: Iran is “ready for real diplomacy,” but “steadfast in the face of any aggression — even if it comes to an apocalyptic war.”
  • NEW — Saudi FM Prince Faisal bin Farhan lands in Washington Monday for talks with Rubio (Reuters) — the mediator track is live on the same day the “this week” round may start. (HIGH)
  • Trump claimed “more than 20 million barrels” moved through Hormuz over the weekend, the largest volume since the war began — his claim, single-source. (MEDIUM on the barrels)
  • The desk’s read: the FM–Rubio readout, not the Truth Social feed, is where the week’s sequence gets set.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The round’s date, venue, and format; whether the rejection is final or leverage.
  • The content of Iran’s “seven conditions”; whether the WSJ post-midterm-strike line is a decision or an aide-level expectation.

Energy · the diesel ban

“WE MAY DO IT” — THE DIESEL BAN IS BACK ON THE TABLE

What we know CONFIRMED

  • At the Presidents Cup Sunday, Trump told Fox News he’s “looking at it very seriously — we may do it” on a diesel export ban (The Times, Bloomberg). That reopens what Sen. Cruz’s Saturday White House assurance had closed. (HIGH)
  • US diesel sits at a record: AAA $6.4531; the $6.5276 record from Sept 22 stands; regular gasoline $4.4768.
  • Goldman math: a ban cuts US diesel ~$0.25/gal per week but raises European wholesale ~$3/gal and US gasoline ~$0.30/gal per week once storage fills. (MEDIUM)
  • Energy Secretary Wright is working with refiners on voluntary export curbs as the alternative; oil CEOs, AFPM, API and the Chamber sent a letter last week urging Trump to abandon the ban. (HIGH)
  • The UK angle: diesel hit a record 199.18p Monday; ministers privately warn of shortages; Britain holds 42 days of reserves; about a third of its diesel imports come from the US. (The Times)
  • VLO, MPC and PSX edged up overnight; diesel futures climbed. Trump blamed the spike on the Russia–Ukraine war and said he told Zelenskyy to “take it easy on the refineries.”
  • The desk’s read: the Saturday assurance vs the Sunday threat is the administration’s indecision on camera. The refiner stocks will price the decision before the market does.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Ban, voluntary curbs, or neither; the timing of any announcement.

  • The desk’s read: Billy’s standing rogue-AI watch just got its strongest verified hit — the agent era’s failure mode is a market event now, not a lab note.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

Trade · Trump–Xi

THE $60B DETAILS LANDED — LISTS REAL, TEETH PENDING

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Late Sunday the White House announced the “30-for-30” framework: $30B each way in tariff cuts on non-sensitive goods (CNN); the truce extended two months to Jan 10. (HIGH)
  • China published its list: 1,619 US items — corn, wheat, sorghum, meat, dairy, veg oils, timber, medical devices, coal — soybeans NOT included, a blow to US farmers. (HIGH)
  • China commits to at least 10M tons of US coal per year in 2027 and 2028 (past commitments “have not always been met in full,” per CNN).
  • Exact tariff rates are unpublished; no cuts are in effect yet (domestic legal processes pending). Greer: improved access for about 30% of US exports to China; the Board of Trade/Investment formally launched.
  • The desk’s read: grade it a B+ — the lists are real, the truce is real, the teeth are pending. The soybean exclusion is the farm-belt tell.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The actual tariff rates and dates; any rare-earth supply restoration; whether China’s coal commitments hold.
