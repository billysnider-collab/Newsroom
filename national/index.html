Iran · the talks happened

Three hours in the room: US–Iran talks happened — and another is scheduled

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The July drought ended: US representatives held three hours of talks with Iranian officials on the UN sidelines Tuesday — Kushner and Witkoff led the US side. Trump: "They had a very good meeting, very productive meeting. And they have another one scheduled in the very near future." The first known face-to-face US–Iran contact since July’s collapsed interim deal (NYPost, Newsmax).
  • After his meeting with British PM Andy Burnham, Trump said: "I think that a settlement is going to be reached." "They want to talk to us. They have been talking to us, even today they’ve been talking to us, and the relationship is developing." (Reuters)
  • The Gulf leaders meeting: Trump told the gathering the Iran deal could come "right after the midterm elections, maybe before" — then threatened to strike Pickaxe Mountain, a suspected Iranian nuclear site: "we’ll blow it up immediately" if the US sees activity (NYPost, CNN). Turkey’s Erdogan, Syria’s al-Sharaa and the Qatari Emir were in the room; the GCC bloc came in urging de-escalation (Bloomberg via NYPost, CNN).
  • Trump’s claims in the room: the US Navy has escorted "more than 1 billion barrels of oil" out of Hormuz, and an Iran deal would send oil prices below prewar levels.
  • The morning’s Reuters exclusive that set the day up: a senior Iranian official told Reuters Iran could reopen Hormuz to Gulf shipping within days if the US lifts its blockade; a September-16 proposal via mediators; the delegation has full authority. Afternoon nuance: Iran’s Fars News Agency said sources denied the reported offer as carried by Kyodo (WSJ) — the exact terms stay foggy.
  • The counter-signal is still on the record: Iran’s military command (Khatam al-Anbia HQ, via Tasnim) says its intelligence suggests a new large-scale US strike is being prepared and warns of "painful" retaliation across the region; countries backing a strike would be treated as parties to the conflict.
  • Brent closed below $100 for the first time since September 8 — $99.25, down 1.1%, the fifth straight down session (WSJ). Mizuho’s Yawger: Trump’s speech "pretty much threw cold water on hopes for a breakthrough" before the midterms.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • What comes out of the scheduled follow-on meeting — no terms disclosed yet.
  • What "lifts its blockade" concretely means — and the contents of the September-16 proposal.
  • The Axios-via-secondhand claim that Iran fired missiles at commercial vessels Monday — single low-quality source, unverified.

Russia-Ukraine · the retaliation landed

Wave one: industrial and energy targets, not cities

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Overnight strikes hit industrial sites in southeastern and central Ukraine: Dnipro, Kryvyi Rih and Pavlohrad in the Dnipropetrovsk region, plus Poltava — 3 killed, 6 injured (Reuters). Ukraine’s air force counted Oniks anti-ship and ballistic missiles, four cruise missiles and 212 drones.
  • Energy is in the mix: a Russian attack on an energy facility left nearly 100,000 consumers in the northern Chernihiv region without power; a "massive" attack hit an industrial enterprise and a critical infrastructure facility in Poltava.
  • This follows Sunday’s Moscow attack and Russia’s vow of intensified strikes (BBC) — the threat is now a wave, with the 24–72 hour retaliation window closing Thursday.
  • The diesel tell holds: US diesel a record $6.527/gal (AAA, vs $3.688 a year ago); Bloomberg: Russia set to extend its diesel export ban; the Moscow refinery’s 2024 throughput was 11.6M tons of crude, 3.2M tons of diesel (Gazprom Neft).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether this is the promised retaliation’s full shape or just the opening wave — and the target set for any second wave.
  • The real Moscow refinery outage duration — Gazprom Neft silent.
  • How Ukraine’s energy-truce offer (see below) interacts with the strike wave.

Houthis · the Yemen toll

WHO: 164 killed in five days; the Saturday operation’s split is the story

What we know CONFIRMED

  • WHO: 164 people killed and 516 injured in Yemen September 13–18; 3,672 casualties since August 6 (AP).
  • Saturday’s Houthi twin operation — "sensitive sites" in Riyadh and Aramco’s Yanbu hub (Al-Masirah); the Saudi coalition says it intercepted the Riyadh ballistic and foiled the rest; AFP-verified video showed flames at an Aramco fuel depot near Riyadh’s King Khalid airport — something got through.
  • Trump told Fox’s Trey Yingst (Sunday) the Houthis have agreed not to attack US troops (per Barron’s) — even as they hit Saudi targets. The split is the story: the Houthis’ war is now explicitly Riyadh’s problem, not Washington’s.

Still unconfirmed HOLD
