FOMC · the decision

The Fed hiked — unanimously. The dots say one more this year

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The Fed raised rates 25bp to 3.75–4.00% on a unanimous vote — the first hike since July 2023 (per Reuters, Barron's, WSJ). The first policy shift under Warsh, who took office in late May after Trump picked him expecting cuts.
  • The dots went hawkish: the median end-2026 outlook rose to 4.1% from 3.8%. 16 of 18 policymakers project at least one more hike this year — 12 see one more, 4 see two more. The median for 2027: no further hikes. Warsh again declined to submit his own projections.
  • At the presser, Warsh refused to give a path: "I'm not in the forward guidance business." He said the decision was not tied to August's CPI: "Trends matter, data points are noisy." And on the market's 91% pricing: "I'll observe market prices and see what they had to say. But today was our decision."
  • Trump's response: "Interest Rates in the United States should be 1%, or less... LOWER THE INTEREST RATES FOR THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA, AND FAST!" on Truth Social — but he backed Warsh personally: "He's a good man, Kevin Warsh, but... he's got a hostile board" (per USA Today, WSJ). NEC director Hassett says the president will accept the hike (per CNN).
  • Warsh on independence: "Independence is a two-way street. We will let people that do trade policy and fiscal policy stay in their lane." He declined to discuss Trump: "I've got nothing for you on a discussion with the president."
  • The CBO's new estimate this week: the Iran war will jack up inflation by ~0.5pp early next year, citing oil/gas disruptions through Hormuz and the Red Sea (per CNN).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • October 28 — the next FOMC meeting, five days before the midterms. Rates markets now call it a coin-flip (per Reuters). How many hikes the dots actually deliver.

Mecca · Tuesday 6:50 PM local

Saudi Arabia says it shot down a Houthi drone headed for Mecca

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Saudi air defenses destroyed a Houthi drone south of Mecca Tuesday at 6:50 PM local, before it entered prohibited airspace (per Reuters, coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki).
  • al-Malki called it the second attempt on the holy city ever — the first since a 2017 ballistic missile (per Reuters).
  • The Houthis deny it — spokesman Yahya Saree says targets were "far removed from the holy places" (per CNN). The 57-nation OIC condemned the attempt.
  • Why it matters: Mecca is the one red line that could unify the Muslim world behind a Saudi war effort and force a US response — Saudi Arabia already asked Washington for direct military help against the Houthis (per Reuters, Sept 12).
  • Whether the drone was actually aimed at Mecca or passing nearby is the difference between an incident and an accelerant.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The drone's actual intended target — dueling claims, unresolved. Whether Washington answers the Saudi request for direct military help.

Energy · Day 6, evening

Pipeline still shut — but the workaround is getting real

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The East-West pipeline is still shut on Day 6 — no official Saudi repair timeline. The US energy secretary says flows "should resume within days"; independent experts say 4–6 weeks (per Reuters/TBS).
  • The workaround is scaling: Kpler tracking shows four VLCCs — about 8 million barrels combined — loading at Ras Tanura on Wednesday, and Energy Aspects says Aramco has doubled daily loadings at Ras Tanura/Juaymah to ~2 VLCCs a day (~4 million bpd, per Reuters).
  • Aramco has offered Arab Light, Medium, and Heavy to Asian term buyers via ship-to-ship transfers off Oman's Sohar port — outside Hormuz (per Reuters, sources). At least one Asian buyer got a Yanbu delay-and-reschedule notice (no length specified).
  • Yanbu loadings remain suspended. The Houthis warned Wednesday that Saudi-linked vessels remain targets in the Red Sea (per Reuters/Stocktwits).
  • Oil gave back the panic premium: Brent fell ~3% to ~$105.35, WTI ~$102.3, after an EIA report showing US crude stocks down 600k barrels but gasoline (+800k) and distillate (+1.6M) builds (per OPIS/EIA). Diesel hit a new US average record: $6.31/gal; European diesel futures hit $210/bbl (per Reuters).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Any official Saudi repair timeline. The first confirmed Sohar ship-to-ship loading — proof the workaround is real. A new Salalah date.

Russia · Ukraine

The "energy truce" confirmed dead — two Rosneft refineries halted

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Trump's Monday claim that both sides "agreed not to hit each other's energy targets" was downgraded by both capitals within hours — and then the strikes resumed in both directions.
  • Overnight Wednesday: Ukraine hit the Syzran refinery in Russia's Volga region — a fire at the primary CDU-6 unit (17,100 tons/day, ~71% of plant capacity, per Reuters sources). Syzran halted processing Tuesday; Saratov suspended operations last Friday. Syzran repairs: at least a month.
  • Ukraine's General Staff confirmed hitting Syzran, a drone production facility in Taganrog, a drone launch site in the Oryol region, and targets in the Black Sea. Zelenskyy: "Russia hasn't stopped striking our energy sector... And our responses to them for this are tangible."
  • The Russia-side toll: overnight, Zelenskyy says a Russian FPV drone struck an intercity minibus near Nikopol — 5 killed, 7 injured. Kyiv took ~80 drones overnight, 63 downed, a warehouse fire in Sviatoshynskyi, 1 injured (per Al Arabiya English citing Klitschko). Tuesday: Russia hit Kyiv petrol stations.
  • Poland commenced military aviation operations as a preventive measure to secure its airspace (per the Polish Armed Forces on X).
  • The ceasefire-enforcement frame: a truce nobody signed isn't a truce — it's a press release both sides bombed through. No monitors, no mechanism, no cost for breaking it.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Damage details at the other struck refineries. Whether the truce idea gets a formal mechanism — HOLD.

DOJ · Tuesday

Five Russian operatives charged in an assassination network on US soil

What we know CONFIRMED

  • DOJ unsealed an indictment Tuesday charging five Russian intelligence operatives with conspiracy to finance terrorism and murder-for-hire: ex-colonel Yuri Khrameev (63), his son Kirill (27, alleged FSB officer), Cubans Oemis Romagoza Durruthy (35) and Yaidel Delgado Suarez (35), and Venezuelan Angel Eduardo Castro (22). All five are at large (per DOJ, Reuters).
  • This summer, a Brooklyn recruit was offered $1,000–1,500 to surveil a Russian dissident believed in the DC area and $40,000 to "eliminate" or "disappear" him — the recruit declined the killing (per DOJ/Reuters/WaEx).
  • Another US recruit was offered $25,000 last year for a killing in Lithuania (per Reuters); the network also commissioned terrorism against civilian and military infrastructure in pro-Ukraine European countries (per DOJ).
  • AG Todd Blanche: "stop America's adversaries from unleashing violence and terror on American soil." Peskov declined comment pending "credible evidence" (per Reuters).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The dissident's identity (DOJ withheld it); whether any plot advanced beyond surveillance. The next indictment tranche — networks like this rarely get fully rolled up in one filing.

NATO · Lithuania

Attribution softened: "most likely not a conscious action"

What we know CONFIRMED

  • President Nausėda said Wednesday the drone downed by an Italian NATO Eurofighter over Lithuania on Tuesday was a Russian Gerbera type most likely aimed at Ukraine — knocked off course by Ukrainian electronic warfare: "most likely not a conscious action against Lithuania" (per Reuters).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

Trump blasted the justices; the mail rule is "a dead letter"

Carry-forwards

China's exit rules take effect; Canada Day 2; VTB waits

What we know CONFIRMED

  • China's exit-and-entry rules took effect Tuesday — citizens violating export controls or deemed tech-security threats can be barred from leaving (6-month to 3-year bans); foreigners face 1–5 year entry bans for false visa statements (per Reuters, WSJ). The first time export-control violations are explicitly linked to exit bans. Read as leverage-setting nine days before the unconfirmed Sept 24 Trump–Xi meeting.
  • Beijing also opened the Pinglu Canal — 134.2 km river-to-sea, 5,000-tonne capacity, its first since 1949 (per MFA spokesperson Mao Ning).
  • Treasury's VTB designation stands; no foreign bank has publicly exited yet — Bessent vowed sanctions on foreign banks "every week" (per Law.com).
  • Canada's expanded 50% Section 338 list is on Day 2; the Sept 29 import bans (dairy, alcohol, motorcycles) are next. Carney: "We have everything we need to pivot and prosper" (per The Times) — Canada is drifting toward the EU as US talks stay collapsed.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Enforcement scope of the exit rules; the first foreign bank to exit VTB; any Sept 29 carve-outs.
