FOMC · Wednesday

Warsh hiked — unanimously. The dots say one more this year

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The FOMC voted 12-0 Wednesday for +25bp to 3.75–4.00% — the first hike since July 2023 and the first rate change since Dec 2025 (per WSJ, Reuters, CNN).
  • Warsh at the 2:30 presser: "Inflation is too high and has been for too long," "this committee will deliver price stability," and "I'm not in the forward guidance business."
  • The SEP pencils in one more hike this year: median end-2026 at 4.1% (from 3.8%); 12 see +25bp more, 4 see +50bp by year-end; no further hikes in 2027. Warsh submitted no dots.
  • Futures now ~50% odds of an October 28 hike — five days before the midterms. Reuters: the market prices three total hikes.
  • Retail sales ran hot Wednesday morning: +1.2% vs +0.8% expected, control group +1.4% — the largest in ~2 years — giving Warsh cover (per Reuters, WSJ). Capital Economics: the economy is "more than capable of handling higher rates."
  • The 10-year closed at 5.003% — the first close above 5% in 19 years; the 30-year at 5.3328% (off the 5.401% 19-year high). Dow -631 (-1.2%) to a 3-month low; S&P -0.4%; Nasdaq ~flat.
  • Trump on Truth Social: rates should be "1%, or less… LOWER THE INTEREST RATES… AND FAST!" To reporters: Warsh is "a good man" but he's got a "hostile board" — the FOMC raises "only for political reasons, and that's a raise against Trump" (per Barron's, Reuters).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Whether October delivers a hike; how Trump escalates — he "may not see another rate cut as president" (per Reuters' McGeever).
  • Whether the 10Y's 5.003% close becomes resistance or a launchpad (ING: 5.25% is the next target).

UN · the findings

"Reasonable grounds" the US committed war crimes in Iran strikes

What we know CONFIRMED

  • A UN-backed mission of experts found "reasonable grounds" to believe the United States committed war crimes in two deadly Iran strikes — including one on a school in the southern city of Minab — and found grounds to believe Tehran committed crimes against humanity (per AP, Reuters).
  • The findings go to the UN Human Rights Council in Geneva. Caption it as "reasonable grounds / possible" — not a verdict.
  • Day 201 of a war Congress voted three times to constrain, and now a UN fact-finding mission is putting the US and Iran in the same paragraph — "reasonable grounds" for war crimes on the US side, crimes against humanity on Tehran's.
  • The story isn't a verdict — it's that the institutional record is being written while the war is still going, which raises the cost of the next strike for everyone.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The US response (no official reaction in the pull); the mission's full report text; whether the HRC acts.

Trade · Canada–EU

Von der Leyen offers Canada a seat; Trump threatens Europe

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Von der Leyen proposed Canada as the EU's first-ever "associate member" in her State of the Union — Carney attended (per CNN, Reuters, WSJ).
  • Trump called it "laughable" and threatened "very serious tariffs" or to stop trading with Europe if he deems it a "hostile act." France's Haddad: the US has no veto over EU choices.
  • Separately, Trump moved to restrict Canadian goods from federal procurement. Context: 50% on ~$20B of Canadian goods last month; Canada's 15–50% retaliation on $20B of US goods Sept 8; Sept 29 import bans pending.
  • Only 20% of Americans support higher Canada tariffs (per Reuters/Ipsos), and Susan Collins calls the policy a "mistake" — the domestic politics are weakening while the trade war spreads to a second front.
  • This is the tariff war's expansion phase: the target pivots to a new partner, and the threat turns on the partner's partner.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • What "associate member" would legally mean; whether the Sept 29 bans get carve-outs.

Washington · Beijing

The GAIN AI Act — chips, China, and a canal

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The GAIN AI Act was introduced in the House hours after Trump said he discussed Nvidia processor access with Xi — it would limit US AI chip exports to China (Rep. Moolenaar, per Bloomberg Law).
  • China opened the Pinglu Canal Wednesday: 134.2 km, ¥72.7B, 5,000-ton vessels — the first national-level river-to-sea canal since 1949 (per TBS).
  • The AI-safety debate is still live in Washington: Altman posted that rapid AI progress could go "very badly" and humans could lose "control of the future," welcomed a federal frontier-AI framework — and said an OpenAI IPO "will not happen in 2026" (per Reuters/Fortune).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • GAIN's committee path and whether it survives contact with the Sept 24 Trump–Xi meeting (still on track per reporting).
