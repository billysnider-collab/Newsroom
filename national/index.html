Iran · Day 210

THE SEVEN-DAY PLAN — TABLED, REJECTED, PRICED, REBOUNCED

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Friday was the fastest diplomacy-to-market cycle of the war. Iran’s FM Abbas Araghchi, at the UN, formally unveiled the seven-day roadmap: release ~$12B in frozen Iranian assets, lift oil sanctions, end the US naval blockade (the June 17 Islamabad MoU, accelerated); all hostilities cease for seven days including Lebanon; once US steps are done (~4–5 days), Hormuz reopens and final-deal talks start on day seven. Conveyed through Qatar; Araghchi met Witkoff Tuesday. "The choice now rests with the United States." (NYT on the contents; Araghchi on the record)
  • Axios reported Trump asked Xi to cut off Iran support; Trump warned Xi against supporting Iran and received assurances China would not do so, per US ambassador David Perdue (MEDIUM); Xi urged a return to the June framework (Xinhua). Trump called the talks "very productive."
  • Midday Friday Brent dropped below $98 and stocks jumped (S&P +0.51%, Dow +479); then the WSJ reported — unnamed US officials — that Trump had rejected the proposal within hours, and oil bounced back to ~$104.32 by the NY close.
  • Trump’s UNGA threat — "annihilate the Islamic Republic" — now reads as the backdrop, not an aside.
  • 80 countries at the UN demanded Hormuz’s urgent reopening and condemned Iran and the Houthis for attacks and trade disruption (Reuters, read out by Bahrain’s FM Thursday).
  • The desk’s read: the plan’s publicness is the story — Tehran wants the world to see it said yes to a ceasefire so a "no" sticks to Washington. But an unnamed-officials rejection is also the cheapest possible "no" — deniable, reversible. Trade the next named voice, not the leak.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Why Trump rejected it — terms? timing? leverage? — and whether the rejection is final or reversible (WSJ sourcing is unnamed US officials: MEDIUM).
  • The reported Wednesday cargo-ship attack in Hormuz that killed a seafarer (thinly sourced); claims of ~40M bbl transiting under US escort (thinly sourced); Salalah; the cargo-seizure bill; Minab stage 3.

Africa · Tigray — "a full war" (day 4)

TIGRAY — "A FULL WAR": THREE AIRPORTS SEIZED, THE GRID BLACKED OUT

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The Sept 23 offensive by the seven-group Ethiopian Peoples’ Forces Alliance for Survival (TPLF/TDF plus Fano, OLA, Afar and others) has widened across three regions in four days. Airports seized at Aksum, Mekelle and Shire; the Afar town of Abala captured; a TDF official called it "full war" — shattering the 2022 Pretoria deal. (Al Jazeera, CNN, Bloomberg, France 24)
  • The ENDF claims 272 Tigrayan fighters killed in Amhara — reported as the army’s claim, not verified.
  • NetBlocks confirmed a subnational internet disruption in the north; Reuters reports the Tigray side itself ordered the blackout to degrade government drone targeting.
  • Ethiopia accused Eritrea of financing the alliance; Eritrea denied it. Israel warned citizens off Tigray; Ethiopian Airlines suspended Mekelle/Axum/Shire flights.
  • The desk’s read: the Tigray-ordered blackout is the tell — they learned from 2020 that drones own the skies, so they’re blinding the grid preemptively. That sophistication means this isn’t a protest gone hot; it’s a planned offensive. Daily line until trajectory is clear.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Casualty counts on either side; the trigger; whether Abala marks a push toward Djibouti’s corridor.

Red Sea · the second front

YANBU IN THE CROSSHAIRS — THE PIPELINE RESTART HAS NO BARRELS YET

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Thursday, the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted six ballistic missiles aimed at Taif and Yanbu (Red Sea); the Houthis claimed strikes on "a sensitive target" in Riyadh and Aramco facilities in Yanbu — no Saudi confirmation of the Riyadh claim.
  • Saudi Civil Defense issued an emergency warning in Mecca. Yemen’s recognized-government VP used UNGA to press world leaders not to trust Houthi assurances.
  • A Khamenei adviser (Mojtaba Khamenei, via AFP/Fars) warned Iran could expand the war to the Indian Ocean if the US or Israel attacks again.
  • Industry sources say Saudi Arabia is building up crude pumping through the East-West pipeline — Day 5 of the reported restart (shut Sept 11 after drone attacks Riyadh blames on Iraqi militia); NO confirmed Yanbu loadings yet.
  • The desk’s read: the Houthis aren’t trying to sink the fleet — they’re trying to make Yanbu uninsurable. If loadings can’t resume, the pipeline restart is a headline, not a barrel.

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • Damage or casualties at Taif or Yanbu; the Saudi/Turkey/Pakistan pact meeting’s outcomes; whether the "US declined Saudi’s request for help" report holds.
