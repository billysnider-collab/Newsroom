Sports desk

Plain English first. If Joe from the bar can’t follow it, we rewrite.

Terms (first use): Odds = the price a sportsbook offers on a bet (how much you win if you’re right). Odd/even = a coin-flip-style market on whether a total (like combined score) finishes odd or even — not a pick of who wins the game. Arbitrage (arb) = betting both sides at two different books when their prices disagree enough that you lock a small profit either way — if both bets get accepted. ROI = return on investment — here, the locked percent profit on the money you put down, before limits and fees. Residuals = the real-world gotchas that can kill the math (bet limits, prices moving, your state blocked, overtime rules).

Two books disagreed on a coin-flip bet

In plain English

DraftKings and BetRivers briefly priced the same odd/even market differently on two Monday CFL games. When that happens, you can sometimes bet both sides — one at each book — and lock a small profit no matter which way the coin lands. That’s not “we know who wins.” It’s “the cash registers disagree.”
