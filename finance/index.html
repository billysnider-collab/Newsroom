In plain English · Mon Sep 7

Jobs came in hotter than expected — and oil stayed expensive after the Iran weekend

Two things hit at once. First: August hiring was much stronger than Wall Street guessed, so people now think the Fed is more likely to raise rates this month. Second: weekend fighting around the Strait of Hormuz kept oil near $97 a barrel — a “war risk” add-on in the price — right as inflation reports land this week. Markets are closed for Labor Day; the next real trade day is Tuesday.

  • Jobs: Employers added about 162,000 jobs in August (most expected ~55,000). Unemployment stayed 4.1%. After that print, betting markets put roughly a coin-flip-plus (~58%) chance the Fed raises rates in September.
  • Oil / Hormuz: After US–Iran tanker strikes, shipping through Hormuz is near May lows. Brent crude ~$97, WTI ~$92 — expensive fuel and freight risk into the week’s inflation data.
  • This week’s calendar: Wednesday — wholesale inflation (PPI) plus after-hours earnings from Oracle and Adobe. Thursday — consumer inflation (CPI) plus Kroger’s call. Fed meeting week comes after.

Coming to terms

Words this card uses — so we’re talking about the same things.

NFP / payrolls
The government’s monthly count of how many jobs employers added or cut. “Hot” means more hiring than expected.
Unemployment rate (U-rate)
Share of people looking for work who don’t have a job. 4.1% here is still historically low.
Fed / rate hike
The Federal Reserve sets a key short-term interest rate. Raising it makes borrowing costlier — meant to cool inflation, can also slow growth.
Fed hike odds
What traders bet the chance is that the Fed raises rates at the next meeting — not a guarantee, just the market’s price.
Strait of Hormuz
A narrow shipping choke point for a huge share of the world’s oil. Trouble there usually lifts oil prices even before barrels go missing.
Hormuz / war-risk premium
The extra dollars in the oil price because traders fear fighting or blocked ships — not just normal supply and demand.
Brent / WTI
Two main oil price benchmarks. Brent is the global yardstick; WTI is the main US one. They’re usually close but not identical.
CPI
Consumer Price Index — the headline “how fast prices are rising for households” report.
PPI
Producer Price Index — prices companies pay/receive earlier in the pipeline; often a preview of consumer inflation.
FOMC / Fed meeting week
The Fed’s policy committee meeting when it decides whether to change rates and what to say about the economy.
Fed blackout
Quiet period before a Fed meeting when officials mostly stop giving speeches — so markets lean harder on data prints.
PMI / ISM services
A survey score of how busy service businesses feel. Above 50 usually means expansion; here services looked strong while some price gauges stayed sticky.
RPO / ARR
Company health shorthand you’ll see around Oracle and Adobe: RPO is contracted future revenue still to be delivered; ARR is annual recurring subscription revenue. We watch them as business-quality checks — not as stock tips.
