Prediction · posted Fri Sept 11, resolves Fri Sept 25

Who owns the bottleneck — the 2-week test

What we know CONFIRMED

  • The hook: August CPI printed "computer software and accessories" up 25.4% year over year — the largest ever recorded (BLS). It looks like software is getting expensive fast.
  • The catch: that number doesn't say what actually got more expensive. The Fed's own researchers note this index isn't quality-adjusted, covers a tiny slice of spending (0.031% weight), and the surge is concentrated in a few months after decades of falling software prices. Don't trade the headline — follow the chain.
  • First order: if AI demand is real, the obvious winners are Nvidia, Microsoft, AMD. That's the trade everyone already sees.
  • Second order: AI needs data moving between chips (Astera Labs, Marvell), optical connections (Coherent, Lumentum), memory (Micron), and cooling for racks that run enormously hot (Vertiv).
  • Third order: the real bottleneck is electricity and physical infrastructure — Eaton, GE Vernova, Amphenol. They don't sell "AI"; they sell what AI physically requires.
  • The prediction: over the next two weeks, the bottleneck basket (equal-weighted ALAB, MRVL, COHR, LITE, MU, VRT, ETN, GEV, APH) outperforms the first-order basket (equal-weighted NVDA, MSFT, AMD), measured from the Sept 11 close to the Sept 25 close. Bottleneck basket ahead = HIT; first-order basket ahead = MISS.
  • This is a thesis test, not investment advice.

Oil · the fork

Brent $108.23 into Monday — the postponement removed the only off-ramp

  • The pipeline is the weekend's genuine market event: 4–5M bpd (4–5% of global supply) offline from the Gulf's only large-scale Hormuz workaround, closed all weekend, with Saudi output already down 23% in August (6.24M bpd, per WSJ via OPEC).

The Fed week

Hike odds ~86% — the bond market has already tightened

What we know CONFIRMED

  • Post-CPI Fed pricing: ~86% odds of a 25bp hike to 3.75–4.00% Wednesday (Reuters Sunday), with JPMorgan's Michael Feroli expecting hikes in both September and December — "failing to back up words with action could put the credibility of the institution at risk."
  • Trump on Sunday, from the Irish Open: the US "should be paying the lowest interest rate in the world" no matter what Fed data indicates; NEC director Kevin Hassett said Trump "will defend the independence of Kevin Warsh above all" (Reuters).
  • Yields are blowing out: 10Y above 4.9% Thursday (highest since 2023); 30Y above 5.38% (nearly a two-decade high); 2Y near 4.6% (14-month high) (Reuters). The bond market has already tightened.
  • Deutsche Bank's Luzzetti: officials may be loath to surprise markets dovish when a hike is ~86% priced — a credibility concern after the July hold sent long-end yields soaring.
  • Synchronized hawkishness: the ECB raised Thursday citing Iran-war energy inflation; the BoJ decides Friday with a hike widely expected; CFTC data Friday shows yen speculators flipped net long for the first time since February (Reuters).

Still unconfirmed HOLD

  • The decision and the vote split — ~86% is odds, not a decision. The risk has moved from the decision to the statement: one hike vs the start of a cycle.
