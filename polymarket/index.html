Think of these like sports lines for news. A price near $0.50 means the crowd is roughly split. Near $0.80 means “most people think yes.” Near $0.05 means “almost nobody thinks yes.” We flag moves and weird books — not what you should buy.

Coming to terms ↓
Price (mid)
Rough “crowd odds” for Yes, in dollars from $0 to $1. $0.60 ≈ “about 60% of the crowd’s money is on Yes.” Not a crystal ball.
Spread (bid vs ask)
Gap between what buyers will pay and sellers will take. Tight (1¢) = clean read. Wide (10¢+) = the number is mushy — don’t trust one figure.
Move today (d1)
How much that mid jumped or dropped in about a day. Big move = something got people’s attention.
Liquidity
How much money is parked near the price. High = harder to shove around. Low = one loud trader can fake a “signal.”
Resolution text
The fine print for what actually counts as Yes. Misreading it is how people invent fake drama.
FACT vs HOLD
FACT = we measured it. HOLD = we won’t pretend we know why, or that the number is a forecast.
Hint
A plain-English “notice this” — still not a bet tip.
Favored / edges Sharp situations — big news, contested books, real money
